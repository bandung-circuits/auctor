Commentary by Joseph Majkut, William Alan Reinsch, Scott Kennedy, Emily Benson, Gracelin Baskaran, Jane Nakano, Quill Robinson. Published May 14, 2024.

On May 14, 2024, the White House announced increased tariffs on Chinese imports across strategic sectors such as steel, aluminum, semiconductors, EVs, batteries, critical minerals, solar cells.

Joseph Majkut (Director, Energy Security and Climate Change): The falling costs of clean energy have been crucial in accelerating the energy transition. Much of these cost reductions have come from China. The new tariffs will raise costs, creating a potential conflict between bolstering domestic industries and rapid decarbonization. CSIS research showed more restrictive trade policies can delay the competitiveness of low-carbon technologies: one year for solar vs natural gas, two years for battery EVs vs ICE vehicles, three years for onshore wind vs gas. The biggest risk is that tariffs become a source of complacency. It is not a suitable strategy for global decarbonization for the United States to make itself an island.

Quill Robinson: Biden raised tariffs on Chinese solar cells from 25% to 50%. Top 10 solar cell manufacturers are based in mainland China and control 84.8% of the global market. Domestically produced panels will come with a significant cost premium.

Jane Nakano: 100% tariff on Chinese EVs and 25% on Chinese lithium-ion EV batteries. Chinese EV factory utilization at only 50% in 2023 vs 80% breakeven. High tariffs also pose danger of fostering complacency among US EV stakeholders.

Gracelin Baskaran: Section 301 demonstrates the delicate balance between national/energy security and cost inflation. If EVs become unaffordable, the domestic auto industry is adversely affected.

Scott Kennedy: Chinese officials argued that the world should welcome growing Chinese production of inexpensive, quality EVs, solar panels, and other goods rather than attacking them as a threat.
