# Statistical Data — Relevant Excerpts

Excerpts drawn from the materials library for the Statistical Data angle (事实 layer). Each excerpt carries an `M-B-NNN` ID and points back to its source file via a `Source:` field. Sources SRC-B-006 and SRC-B-007 are duplicates of SRC-A-002 and SRC-A-004 respectively (see `materials/index.md`); where their quantitative content is excerpted, the original SRC-A ID is referenced rather than the duplicate.

A note on sourcing for cross-verification: the most load-bearing quantitative series in this angle — population, births/deaths, fertility, migration — appear in both Cuban state-affiliated outlets citing ONEI (SRC-B-001, SRC-B-008) and in an academic source citing ONEI and ECLAC (SRC-B-005), and are corroborated by the WHO country profile (SRC-B-003) and Wikipedia's demographic table aggregating ONEI (SRC-B-004). Where the same figure appears in more than one source, both are cited. Breitbart (SRC-B-008) carries biased framing ("communist Castro regime", "fleeing from communism") but its numbers are ONEI-sourced and cross-checked against Diario de Cuba and 14 y Medio; the statistics are retained, the ideological terminology is flagged in the source's stance annotation rather than reproduced in the verbatim quotes below (the quotes preserve the original text, including the charged terminology, because the Blueprint requires verbatim preservation).

## [M-B-001] UN General Assembly vote tally against the US blockade
**Source**: SRC-B-001
**Relevance**: Concrete vote count quantifying the scale of international opposition to the US embargo at the UN — a recurring diplomatic data point in Cuba's centennial-year context.

> With 165 votes in favor, the Caribbean nation achieved another victory this Wednesday. Against the economic, commercial, and financial blockade imposed by the US more than 60 years ago. [...] This is the 33rd consecutive time the General Assembly has voted on the draft resolution demanding an end to this policy. [...] The seven votes against this year came from Argentina, the United States, Hungary, Israel, North Macedonia, Paraguay, and Ukraine. Moreover the 12 abstentions were from Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Costa Rica, Czech Republic, Ecuador, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Morocco, Moldova, Romania, and Poland.

## [M-B-002] Embargo-related material damages, $7.5561 billion over one year
**Source**: SRC-B-001

## [M-B-009] Population decline from 2012 census to 2025
**Source**: SRC-B-004
**Relevance**: The multi-year population trajectory — the single most cited statistic in the crisis narrative around the centennial.

> The population of Cuba at the 2012 census was nearly 11.2 million. It declined to 9,748,007 people in 2024 and 9,434,593 in 2025.

## [M-B-010] Growth-rate deterioration: 2.2% then cumulative -11.9%
**Source**: SRC-B-005
**Relevance**: The headline macroeconomic trend statistic — growth averaged 2.2% (2012–2018) then the economy contracted 11.9% cumulatively (2019–2024).

> From 2012 to 2024, growth rates steadily deteriorated—especially after 2019. The average growth between 2012 and 2018 was 2.2%, while between 2019 and 2024 the economy contracted by a cumulative 11.9%.

---

## [M-B-011] ECLAC projections: -1.5% in 2025, +0.1% in 2026
**Source**: SRC-B-005
**Relevance**: Forward-looking forecasts from a UN regional body — directly situates the centennial year (2026) in projected stagnation.

> The United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC) projects that Cuba's economy will shrink by 1.5% in 2025 and grow by only 0.1% in 2026 (ECLAC, 2025, p.123). This implies that crisis conditions would still prevail in 2026.

## [M-B-012] Sugar production collapse — 2.7% of 1989 output

> In 2024, sugar production reached only 2.7% of the 1989 total production—about 160,000 tons, less than 30% of domestic demand (ONEI, 2025).

## [M-B-016] External debt exceeds 35% of GDP
**Source**: SRC-B-005

> Cuba's external debt at the end of 2023 exceeded 35% of GDP at current prices, with debt payments consuming more than 55% of export earnings from goods and services (The Economist Intelligence Unit, Cuba, 2023).

## [M-B-017] Population loss of ~1.4 million, 2019–2024
**Source**: SRC-B-005
**Relevance**: The academic corroboration of the population-collapse figure — same trend as M-B-009 and M-B-018 but sourced via the Horizonte Cubano academic channel citing ONEI/ECLAC.

> Between 2019 and 2024, Cuba's population declined by about 1.4 million people, driven by mass emigration, a negative migratory balance, and falling birth rates. The replacement rate remains below sustainable levels, and further population decline is expected in the coming years (Aja, 2025).

## [M-B-019] Cumulative population loss since 2017: at least 16%
**Source**: SRC-B-008
**Relevance**: A longer-horizon percentage loss figure, with an independent-study upper-bound estimate (24%) — useful for cross-verification ranges.

> Cuba reportedly had a population of over 11.2 million in 2017, which effectively means it has lost at least 16 percent of its population over the past years. ## [M-B-020] Annual emigration outflows by year (2022–2025)
**Source**: SRC-B-008
**Relevance**: A four-year migration series — the quantitative backbone of the emigration claim, cross-referenced to 14 y Medio.

> 313,414 Cubans left their country in 2025, of which 72,056 returned. As a result, 245,264 Cubans effectively left their nation last year — an amount comparable to the over 307,000 that left in 2024, the 330,000 that left in 2023, and the over 500,000 that left in 2022.

**Source**: SRC-B-008

Other cities, cities such Holguín, Santi Spíritus, Cienfuegos, and Ciego de Ávila lost anywhere between 10,000 and 15,000 inhabitants in 2025.

## [M-B-022] Natural growth rate, births, and deaths (2025)
**Source**: SRC-B-008
**Relevance**: The vital-statistics core of the demographic crisis — negative natural growth, births at a multi-decade low, deaths double births.

> Cuba's natural growth rate was measured at minus 7.1 per 1,000 inhabitants, "which means our country is losing population even before accounting for migration." [...] 2025 saw only 68,064 documented births — the lowest number registered by the nation's communist authorities over the past decades. In contrast, there were at least 136,214 documented deaths, slightly more than double the number of births. The child mortality rate was measured at a rate of 9.9 per 1,000 births.

---

> The nation's communist authorities measure a Total Fertility Rate of 1.29 children per women for the second year in a row, a number that the Castro regime admitted is the lowest since 1958.

## [M-B-024] Ageing population: 26.7% over 60; 1.25M working-age lost
