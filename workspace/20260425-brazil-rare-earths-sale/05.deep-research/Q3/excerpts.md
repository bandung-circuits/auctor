# Non-Alignment under Structural Asymmetry & Alternative Development Models — Research Excerpts

## Question: How does the 15-year exclusive offtake agreement's structure effectively negate Brazil's declared "non-alignment" position?

### [Q3-001] 15-Year 100% Offtake Lock-In: All Output Committed to US-Government-Capitalized SPV
**Source**: SRC-A-005 [existing]
**Relevance**: The press release provides the definitive terms of the offtake agreement — 100% of Phase 1 production for 15 years, with a SPV capitalized by US government agencies and private capital, including guaranteed minimum floor prices for Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb.

> Serra Verde has secured a 15-year, 100% offtake agreement to supply a special purpose vehicle capitalized by various U.S. Government agencies, as well as private capital sources (the "SPV") with all four of the magnetic rare earths that are required to make a permanent NdFeB magnet. This offtake includes guaranteed minimum floor prices for each of Nd, Pr, Dy and Tb, which helps to de-risk cash flows and provides access to shared upside.

### [Q3-002] Exclusivity Clause Prohibits Sale to Brazilian Enterprises
**Source**: SRC-F-009 [existing] (M-F-024)
**Relevance**: Tricontinental Institute analysis reveals the offtake's exclusivity clause prohibits sale to any company outside the SPV, including Brazilian enterprises — directly negating Brazil's non-alignment claim.

> Chama a atenção de qualquer leitor mais atento o teor da cláusula de proibição de venda da produção da Serra Verde para qualquer empresa do resto do mundo, inclusive para empresas brasileiras.

### [Q3-003] Brazil Declares It Will Avoid Exclusive Agreements — Contradicted by Existing Deal
**Source**: SRC-Q3-005 [new]
**Relevance**: The Brazilian government explicitly declared it would avoid exclusivity agreements on critical minerals — yet Serra Verde's offtake agreement is precisely an exclusivity agreement committing all output to US-aligned channels for 15 years.

> According to a representative of the Brazilian government interviewed by BNamericas, on condition of anonymity, Brazil is not interested in entering into any kind of exclusivity agreement with any country to develop its critical minerals. "I consider the Brazilian government's strategy to be correct, because you have to seek the best market solutions for the development of the critical minerals chain and with the largest possible number of participants."

### [Q3-004] Lula's "No Veto, No Preference" Position vs. 15-Year Exclusivity Reality
**Source**: SRC-B-006 [existing] (M-I-013)
**Relevance**: Lula publicly declared Brazil has "no veto" on US participation and "no intention to offer preferential access" — yet the 15-year exclusive offtake creates precisely that: preferential, exclusive access for US-aligned entities.

> "USA Rare Earth's proposed $2.8 billion acquisition gives Washington a route into Brazilian production. But Lula's message after meeting Trump was clear: Brazil has 'no veto' over US participation, yet also no intention to offer preferential access." "This means Brazil can become a strategic rare earths supplier to the US -- but not quickly nor on US terms alone."

### [Q3-005] Durans' "Friends with All Blocs" Stance vs. Output Lock-In
**Source**: SRC-F-004 [existing] (M-F-007, M-I-011)
**Relevance**: Brazil's official non-alignment stance — "friends with all countries from every bloc" — is structurally contradicted when 100% of the country's rare earth output is locked into a US-aligned supply chain for 15 years.

> Durans said Brazil's historical policy is to "be friends with all countries from every bloc", and added that the country will not take a side with the US or China. "We want to receive this capital that wants to invest in the country but with the counterproposal of joint technological development, so we can have a win-win between Brazil and the US, with the EU or with China."

**Assessment**: This question is fully answered by existing and new materials. The 15-year 100% exclusive offtake negates Brazil's non-alignment through multiple structural mechanisms: (1) exclusive lock-in of all Phase 1 output to US-government-capitalized SPV; (2) prohibition on sale to Brazilian enterprises or any entity outside the SPV; (3) US government equity option via DFC financing; (4) price floors backed by US government agencies; (5) Trump administration's $1.6 billion equity stake exceeding 15% in USAR. The contradiction is stark: Brazil declares "no exclusivity agreements" and "friends with all blocs" while its only producing rare earth mine has all output committed exclusively to US-aligned channels for a generation.

## Question: What would a genuinely non-aligned framework for rare earth development look like — are there existing models?

### [Q3-006] Indonesia's Nickel Export Ban: A Non-Aligned Downstream Industrialization Model
**Source**: SRC-Q3-001 [new]
**Relevance**: Indonesia provides the most concrete existing model of a non-aligned downstream industrialization strategy: banning raw ore exports and attracting processing investment from multiple partners including Chinese firms, rather than committing output exclusively to one bloc.

> Indonesia's 2020 decision to halt the export of nickel ore is a continuation of its industrial policy to produce downstream materials and products in the nickel and EV battery supply chain. In 2014, Indonesia successfully employed a similar export ban to develop downstream value-added nickel products for the stainless-steel supply chain. Aided by China's large demand for stainless steel, Indonesia created a well-developed intermediate nickel product and stainless-steel industry. In 2021, it was the second-largest producer of stainless steel in the world. The motivation of the new law is to reinforce the industry while also trying to replicate this success in the EV battery market.
>
> Indonesia's strategy forced Chinese EV companies, and by extension the Chinese government, to switch from importing nickel ore to assisting in the development of HPAL/MHP production facilities in Indonesia. China is deploying a hybrid strategy that secures its supply of nickel products while maintaining a degree of control. Through its investment in HPAL/MHP technology and JVs in Indonesia, China secures a supply of MHP and relieves a supply chain bottleneck. Even though the JVs must share future profits with Indonesian partners, China avoids increased demand on its strained energy grid.

### [Q3-007] China Willing to Build Processing Where the Ore Is — Key to Non-Aligned Approach
**Source**: SRC-J-007 [existing] (M-J-011)
**Relevance**: PIIE analysis confirms that China has demonstrated willingness to relocate processing capacity to ore-producing countries when those countries impose domestic processing mandates — this is the core mechanism for a genuinely non-aligned framework.

> China would be eager to work with Brazil, Hendrix said, citing the example of Indonesia, which banned the export of unprocessed nickel ore in 2020. Its move prompted Chinese firms to relocate their processing facilities to Indonesia itself, moving in behind the export ban and shifting midstream activity to where the ore was being extracted. Hendrix cited this as evidence that China "has been willing to negotiate and meet the ore producers where they are," conditional on those countries being large enough to have real bargaining power. "Brazil, with its continental scale and diversified export base, clearly meets that threshold."

### [Q3-008] US-Driven Frameworks Undermine Global South Value Addition
**Source**: SRC-Q3-004 [new]
**Relevance**: Dialogue Earth analysis shows that US-led critical minerals frameworks (Forge, bilateral deals) do not address Global South countries' primary objective of moving up the value chain, and may actually undermine regional coordination efforts.

> "A multilateral trading bloc doesn't seem to move the needle on producer country ambitions or bottlenecks to value addition," says Thomas Scurfield, Africa senior economic analyst at the NRGI. In the African context, regional arrangements would be key to value addition, allowing countries to pool their respective strengths. Scurfield says the US emphasis on bilateral deals and a multilateral trading bloc could undermine or complicate those efforts.
>
> "The deeper question is whether access to US-led initiatives will depend on geopolitical alignment, potentially forcing countries to reconsider long-standing economic ties with China."

### [Q3-009] Alternative Non-Aligned Models: Minerals Sovereignty Alliance, Domestic Processing Mandates
**Source**: SRC-J-004 [existing] (M-J-007)
**Relevance**: Proposes concrete alternative frameworks that Global South nations could pursue instead of exclusive bloc-aligned supply chains.

> Form a 'Minerals for Sovereignty' alliance among Global South nations (e.g., Brazil, Indonesia, DRC) to collectively negotiate prices and processing standards, reducing Western corporate leverage. Model this after OPEC but with a focus on equitable terms of trade, including technology transfer and local employment guarantees. Push for a UN treaty on critical minerals that mandates environmental and social safeguards.
>
> Build small-scale, modular processing facilities in the Global South powered by solar/wind, reducing reliance on China's centralized refineries and lowering transportation emissions.

### [Q3-010] Brazil's 80% Domestic Processing Mandate — A Policy Instrument for Non-Aligned Framework
**Source**: SRC-B-005 [existing] (M-J-014)
**Relevance**: Brazil's PNMCE bill mandates at least 80% domestic processing of critical strategic minerals, effectively banning raw ore exports — the core policy instrument of a genuinely non-aligned framework (similar to Indonesia's approach).

> Under the National Policy for Critical and Strategic Minerals (PNMCE, Bill PL 4.443/2025), at least 80% of critical strategic minerals must be processed domestically, effectively banning raw ore exports.

**Assessment**: This question is partially answered. Indonesia provides the most concrete existing model, and the analysis explains why it works (export ban forces processing investment to relocate domestically). Brazil's own 80% domestic processing mandate replicates this approach. However, no genuinely non-aligned framework for rare earths specifically exists in practice — Indonesia's model is for nickel, and the rare earth sector has different structural characteristics (more complex processing, more concentrated market). The "Minerals for Sovereignty" alliance proposal and domestic processing mandates are theoretical frameworks, not yet operational models. Gap remains: no existing operational non-aligned rare earth framework found.

## Question: What were the specific policy steps Indonesia took in its nickel export ban and downstream industrialization strategy?

### [Q3-011] Indonesia's Nickel Export Ban Timeline: 2009 Mining Law, 2014 First Ban, 2017 Relaxation, 2020 Permanent Ban
**Source**: SRC-Q3-001 [new], supplemented by search results from IISD case study
**Relevance**: Provides the specific policy timeline of Indonesia's nickel downstream industrialization strategy.

> Indonesia's 2020 decision to halt the export of nickel ore is a continuation of its industrial policy to produce downstream materials and products in the nickel and EV battery supply chain. In 2014, Indonesia successfully employed a similar export ban to develop downstream value-added nickel products for the stainless-steel supply chain. Aided by China's large demand for stainless steel, Indonesia created a well-developed intermediate nickel product and stainless-steel industry. In 2021, it was the second-largest producer of stainless steel in the world.

IISD case study timeline (from search results):
- 2009: Mining Law mandates domestic processing of minerals
- 2014: First full nickel ore export ban (Government Regulation No. 1/2017 and 2014 ban)
- 2017: Temporary relaxation — limited exports allowed for miners building smelters
- 2020: Permanent ban reinstated, two years ahead of original schedule (moved from 2022 to January 1, 2020 via MEMR Regulation)

### [Q3-012] Indonesia's Two-Step Strategy: First Nickel Pig Iron/Stainless Steel, Then EV Batteries
**Source**: SRC-Q3-001 [new]
**Relevance**: Indonesia pursued a deliberate two-step industrialization strategy — first developing nickel pig iron and stainless steel capacity (Class 2 nickel processing), then targeting EV battery cathode production (HPAL/MHP technology).

> Indonesia's strategy will require a multiyear two-step process of technological upgrades. First, Indonesia must develop the ability to economically refine Class 2 nickel on a large scale into MHP. Next, it must create the industrial processes and infrastructure and investments to transform MHP into cathodes and EV batteries.
>
> Despite these obstacles, the strategy has demonstrated some success as several MHP investment projects have been announced. Chinese companies are involved in many of the projects. These include Halmahera Persada Lygend, a joint venture between Indonesia's Harita Group and China's Ningbo Lygend and Huayue; and a JV of Huayou, Tsingshan Holding Group, and China Molybdenum Co.

### [Q3-013] Smelter Construction Requirements and Tax on Intermediate Products
**Source**: SRC-Q3-001 [new]
**Relevance**: Indonesia used smelter construction requirements as conditions for any export relaxation, and is considering taxes on intermediate nickel products (like MHP) to force further downstream investment.

> The 2017 relaxation allowed limited exports only for miners who were building smelters — ensuring that any temporary export permission came with investment commitments. The Widodo administration is considering a tax on the export of intermediate nickel products such as MHP that would force the decision for all countries to move battery production to Indonesia.

### [Q3-014] Indonesia's Results: 55 Nickel Processing Facilities, Second-Largest Stainless Steel Producer
**Source**: Search results (WRI technical perspective, people-also-ask data)
**Relevance**: Quantifies the concrete outcomes of Indonesia's downstream industrialization strategy.

> Indonesia has 55 nickel processing facilities in operation. The vast majority, 90%, use smelting. In 2021, Indonesia was the second-largest producer of stainless steel in the world.

**Assessment**: This question is well answered. The specific policy steps are: (1) 2009 Mining Law mandating domestic processing; (2) 2014 first nickel ore export ban; (3) 2017 temporary relaxation conditional on smelter construction; (4) 2020 permanent ban reinstated ahead of schedule; (5) smelter construction requirements tied to any export permission; (6) consideration of tax on intermediate products to force further downstream investment. Results: 55 processing facilities, second-largest stainless steel producer, multiple HPAL/MHP projects with Chinese JV partners. A small gap remains: more detailed documentation of the specific regulatory instruments (Government Regulation numbers, ministerial decrees) would strengthen the answer.

## Question: How has Shenghe Resources and other Chinese private-sector REE entities responded to losing Serra Verde's output?

### [Q3-015] Serra Verde Renegotiated and Shortened Chinese Offtake Agreements in December 2025
**Source**: SRC-I-001 [existing] (M-I-015), SRC-Q3-002 [new]
**Relevance**: Documents the specific mechanism by which Shenghe lost Serra Verde's output — the renegotiation and dramatic shortening of decade-long offtake agreements.

> In December 2025, Serra Verde renegotiated and dramatically shortened its Chinese offtake agreements — originally expected to run roughly a decade — so that they now expire at the end of 2026. That move places Serra Verde alongside MP Materials (and more recently VHM Ltd) in stepping away from long-term dependence on Shenghe Resources.

### [Q3-016] Shenghe Acquired Peak Rare Earths and Ngualla Project in Tanzania — Alternative Feedstock
**Source**: SRC-Q3-002 [new], supplemented by Reuters/Shenghe acquisition search results
**Relevance**: Shenghe's response to losing Serra Verde's output has been to lock up alternative upstream feedstock through acquisition of Peak Rare Earths and the Ngualla project in Tanzania in September 2025.

> Shenghe finalized its acquisition of Peak Rare Earths and the Ngualla project in Tanzania in September 2025 -- a reminder that China continues to lock up upstream optionality even as some downstream contracts unwind.
>
> The Ngualla Project represents one of the world's largest and highest-grade undeveloped rare earth deposits. Shenghe's acquisition allows it to implement proprietary processing technologies across the entire value chain, from mining through to the production of rare earth oxides and metals. The company plans to make a final investment decision in July 2026 and has signed offtake agreements with Hyundai, Siemens, and others.

### [Q3-017] Pattern of Western Firms Moving Away from Shenghe Dependence
**Source**: SRC-Q3-002 [new], SRC-I-001 [existing] (M-I-015)
**Relevance**: Serra Verde is not alone in moving away from Shenghe — MP Materials and VHM Ltd have also stepped away, creating a broader pattern of Chinese offtake unwinding.

> That move places Serra Verde alongside MP Materials (and more recently VHM Ltd) in stepping away from long-term dependence on Shenghe Resources. Over the past year, that shift has become a defining pattern as Western capital offers more attractive financing and faster downstream alignment.

### [Q3-018] Serra Verde Previously Sent All Ore to Shenghe for 14 Years Without Objection
**Source**: SRC-H-007 [existing] (M-H-011)
**Relevance**: Contextualizes Shenghe's loss — for 14 years, Serra Verde sent all its ore to Shenghe without any Brazilian objection, meaning Shenghe had a stable arrangement that was disrupted only when Western financing became available.

> Por 14 anos, a Serra Verde exportou praticamente todo o seu minério bruto para a Shenghe Resources (empresa parcialmente estatal chinesa sediada em Chengdu, fundada em 1998). Durante os governos de Lula, Dilma, Temer e Bolsonaro, não houve qualquer objeção à entrega de matéria-prima de baixo valor agregado à China.

**Assessment**: This question is partially answered. The response mechanism is documented: Shenghe acquired alternative upstream feedstock (Peak/Ngualla in Tanzania). The broader pattern of Western firms moving away from Shenghe is established. However, a significant gap remains: Shenghe's own public statements or commercial strategy documents about the loss of Serra Verde output have not been found. Other Chinese private-sector REE entities' responses (e.g., China Northern Rare Earth, Ganzhou Rare Earth Group) to the broader Western supply chain restructuring are not documented. Chinese commercial media coverage beyond state-linked outlets (Sina Finance, GlobalTechMap) was not accessible.

## Question: What is the current status and technical viability of Brazil's MagBras (Magnet Bras) initiative?

### [Q3-019] MagBras: 38 Companies, R$73 Million Budget, Target 2030 for Industrial-Scale Production
**Source**: SRC-Q3-003 [new]
**Relevance**: Provides the most detailed account of MagBras' scope, budget, participant count, and timeline.

> The CIT SENAI ITR is a part of the project MagBras: From Mine to Magnet, approved after a call under the Brazilian federal government's Green Mobility and Innovation program. Launched in July 2025, the initiative brings together 38 companies, start-ups, innovation centers, universities, research institutions, and support foundations. With a budget of R$73 million, of which R$60 million is provided by the federal government and the remainder cofunded by the participating entities, the program is aimed at consolidating the Brazilian productive chain for permanent magnets from rare earth mining to final product fabrication.
>
> "MagBras is a technical alliance forged with this aim, and we are looking to achieve this objective by 2030."

### [Q3-020] Pilot Scale Mastery, 60-70% Industrial Level — Key Equipment Imported from China
**Source**: SRC-Q3-003 [new]
**Relevance**: Documents the specific technical progress and key dependency: pilot-scale mastery achieved but 60-70% at industrial level, with critical equipment imported from China.

> "On a pilot scale, we have already mastered the technological production cycle for permanent magnets made from neodymium [Nd], iron [Fe], and boron [B]. On an industrial level we have mastered between 60% and 70% of the process," says Andre Pimenta de Faria, who runs the CIT SENAI ITR. "We need to conclude the commissioning [real-time operational testing] of some equipment items, all imported from China."

### [Q3-021] Separation Bottleneck: CETEM Separated Pr/Nd Using Synthetic Solutions Only
**Source**: SRC-Q3-003 [new]
**Relevance**: Documents the critical technical bottleneck: Brazil's separation capability uses synthetic (not real) rare earth concentrates, and requires minimum 20 kg of real concentrate to test with actual Brazilian minerals.

> Chemical engineer Ysrael Marrero Vera says that the center has successfully separated praseodymium (Pr) and neodymium. "We separate these materials using what we call synthetic solutions, and not with real chemical rare earth mineral concentrate, because for the time being we don't produce that." The process was registered with INPI, patent granted in 2022. Vera explains that to chemically separate rare earths, a minimum of 20 kilograms of chemical rare earth concentrate is required, and very few companies in Brazil are currently capable of providing this quantity.

### [Q3-022] Magnet Grade Gap: 42 MGOe at Lab Scale vs. 55 MGOe Required for Commercial
**Source**: SRC-Q3-003 [new]
**Relevance**: Quantifies the key performance gap between laboratory and commercial scale magnets.

> Wendhausen believes the biggest challenge comes in obtaining high magnetic property values. On a laboratory scale, the UFSC group was able to produce magnets with a grade of 42 megagauss-oersteds (MGOe) -- to achieve commercial scale, this value must be 55 MGOe. "We conduct our tests on smaller devices of lower power, such as small motors. The material we develop is not suitable for application in bigger machines such as wind turbines."

### [Q3-023] First 20 kg of Domestic Rare Earth Carbonate Delivered to CIT SENAI
**Source**: SRC-B-005 [existing] (M-J-014), supplemented by omaringa.com.br news
**Relevance**: Documents the milestone of first autonomous full-process production from Brazilian minerals.

> MagBras Initiative: Led by CIT SENAI in Minas Gerais and coordinated by FIESC in Santa Catarina, this project united 28 companies and research bodies to deliver the first 20kg of rare earth carbonate. This marked Brazil's first autonomous, full-process production from mining to chemical compound.
>
> The carbonate was obtained from ionic clays extracted from Pocos de Caldas Plateau. Approximately 2 kilograms of the intermediate compound can be produced from about 600 kilograms of clay. The carbonate produced has concentrations of rare earth elements that can reach 98 percent, in addition to recovery rates close to 79 percent, figures considered superior to the average observed in international operations.

### [Q3-024] MagBras as Industrial Demonstrator — Lab Factory Not Intended for Commercial Production
**Source**: SRC-Q3-003 [new], SRC-Q3-003 [new]
**Relevance**: Clarifies that MagBras' CIT SENAI facility is a research center and technology demonstrator, not a commercial production facility — the aim is technology transfer to private companies.

> The maximum output capacity of the unit is 100 tonnes per year of permanent magnets, but as it is also a research center, this volume will not likely be achieved. "It is not our intention to commercially fabricate these magnets -- as a research institute we cannot do that," explains Faria. "We are seeking to master the productive chain of these components and then transfer technology to companies interested in producing the magnets in Brazil."

**Assessment**: This question is well answered. MagBras' current status is: (1) pilot-scale mastery of NdFeB magnet production cycle achieved; (2) 60-70% of industrial-level process mastered; (3) first 20 kg of domestically produced rare earth carbonate delivered from Pocos de Caldas ionic clays with 98% concentration and 79% recovery rates; (4) 38 companies and R$73 million budget; (5) target 2030 for full industrial-scale chain. Key viability challenges: (a) separation technology proven only with synthetic solutions, not real Brazilian concentrates; (b) magnet grade gap: 42 MGOe lab vs. 55 MGOe commercial requirement; (c) critical equipment imported from China; (d) raw materials for magnet production still imported from China; (e) minimum 20 kg real concentrate needed for separation testing, few providers exist; (f) facility is a research/demonstrator center, not commercial producer — technology must be transferred to private companies.
