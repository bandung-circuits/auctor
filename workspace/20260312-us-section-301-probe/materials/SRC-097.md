# [SRC-097] China can decarbonise the world -- but even that won't fix its overcapacity problem

**Source URL**: https://www.bruegel.org/analysis/china-can-decarbonise-world-even-wont-fix-its-overcapacity-problem
**Fetch Time**: 2026-03-12T22:25:00Z
**Source Type**: Think Tanks and Research Institutes (Bruegel)
**Credibility**: High

**Original Content**:

Analysis by Alicia Garcia-Herrero and Haoxin Mu. Published 24 September 2025.

China is crucial to the world's green transition: it is the world's largest greenhouse gas emitter (over 30% of global total) AND the world's largest producer of green technology, manufacturing 92% of the world's solar modules and 82% of wind turbines as of 2024. China aims for emissions peak by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2060. Non-fossil fuel share of energy consumption: 19.8% in 2024 (target: 20% by 2025, 25% by 2030, 80% by 2060).

Despite being behind schedule, global installation of renewable power is not too far from the net-zero 2050 trajectory. China is surpassing the required rate while the rest of world moves too slowly. In 2024, China supplied 91% of solar modules and 39% of wind turbines installed outside China. China can already produce enough renewable products for the world's energy transition, even in the most aggressive scenario. China faces overcapacity because global demand remains far below the NZE 2050 level.

Chinese solar overcapacity has grown since 2023 as export prices fell while volumes rose. Profitability of Chinese solar manufacturers plummeted -- 200% fall in operating income 2022-2024. From 2020-2024, China installed 900 GW of renewable capacity yet failed to reduce energy and carbon intensity as planned due to widespread underutilisation and increased curtailment caused by underinvestment in the power grid. Investment in power generation surged while grid investment remained stagnant.

Conclusion: China needs to pivot from manufacturing to grid infrastructure investment. Power infrastructure may replace renewable equipment as a new trade revenue source, as it is less sensitive to tariffs/sanctions.
