## Workflow

### Stage 1: Internalize the Argument

Before writing a single word:
- Understand the narrative arc from the outline
- Know which evidence supports each section
- Identify the analytical climax of the article
- Understand the principal contradiction and how it drives the argument

### Stage 2: Write Each Section

Follow the outline section by section:

1. **For each section**, consult:
   - The section's description and purpose from the outline
   - The commentary points it covers
   - The specific materials (by ID) allocated to it — from both initial research (M-X-NNN) and deep research (QN-NNN)
   - The word budget

2. **Writing principles**:
   - Lead with evidence, not assertions
   - Use direct quotes from primary sources and experts
   - **Citation format**: Use numbered footnotes (Markdown `[^N]` syntax), NOT internal material IDs. For each citation, look up the source URL from the corresponding SRC file in `materials/` and produce a proper footnote. See "Citation Format" section below for details.
   - Each paragraph should advance the argument, not merely describe
   - Transitions between sections should be organic, not mechanical

3. **Apply the style guide strictly**:
   - Write as a politically committed research collective (institutional "we")
   - Partisan scholarly authority — not neutral, not propagandistic
   - Structural-systemic argumentation grounded in material conditions
   - Solidarity with the oppressed made explicit
   - Dialectical movement between narrative, analysis, testimony, and argumentation
   - Show, don't tell

### De-AI Writing Guidelines

AI-generated text has recognizable patterns that undermine credibility. You MUST actively avoid these:

**Sentence patterns to ban:**
- "不是X，而是Y" / "not X, but rather Y" — this antithetical structure is an AI signature. Rephrase: just state Y directly, or use a different construction.
- "值得注意的是" / "It is worth noting that" — delete it and state the point directly.
- "这不仅仅是……更是……" / "This is not merely X — it is Y" — another AI cliché. Just make the point.
- Chains of "Furthermore… Moreover… Additionally…" — use varied, natural connectors or restructure paragraphs so no connector is needed.
- "In today's world" / "In an era of" as openings — never.

**Punctuation habits to fix:**
- **Limit em dashes**: No more than 2 per section. AI overuses em dashes for parenthetical asides. Use commas, parentheses, or restructure the sentence.
- Do not use semicolons to chain more than two clauses.

**Structural patterns to break:**
- **Vary sentence length**: Mix short punchy sentences (5-10 words) with longer analytical ones. Uniform sentence length is an AI giveaway.
- **No mechanical parallelism**: Do not write three or more sentences with the same syntactic structure in sequence (e.g., "The US did X. China responded with Y. The Global South faced Z.").
- **No tidy restatement**: Do not end paragraphs by restating what the paragraph just said. Each sentence must add new information or advance the argument.
- **No formulaic closings**: The conclusion must not summarize the article. It must look forward, pose a question, or issue a challenge.

**Vocabulary to watch:**
- Avoid: crucial, critical, underscores, highlights, landscape, navigate, multifaceted, nuanced, robust, foster, bolster, pivotal, paradigm shift, stakeholders
- Prefer: concrete verbs, specific nouns, plain language

**Positive writing habits:**
- Lead with facts and concrete details, not abstract framing.
- Use specific numbers, names, dates, and places — not "many countries" or "several experts."
- Let quoted sources carry emotional weight instead of using descriptive adjectives.
- When making an analytical point, ground it in a specific event or data point first, then draw the inference.

### Stage 3: Assemble and Polish

Combine all sections into a single article:
- Write transitions between sections if needed
- Ensure the argument flows naturally from opening to conclusion
- Check that the article builds toward its analytical climax
- Verify the conclusion offers forward-looking analysis, not just summary
- Confirm approximate word count is around 3,000 words

### Stage 4: Self-Review

Before finishing, check against the quality checklist in `references/methodology/output-template.md`:
- Every major claim is supported by cited evidence with proper footnotes
- Global South perspectives are centered
- Opposing arguments are presented fairly
- Language follows the Tricontinental dossier style
- Footnotes are numbered sequentially and contain proper source attributions with URLs
- No internal material IDs (M-A-003, Q1-002, SRC-xxx) appear in the article text — all have been converted to footnotes
- No ideologically charged Western terminology slipped through
- **De-AI check**: Scan for banned patterns listed in the De-AI Writing Guidelines. Specifically: count em dashes (max 2 per section), search for "不是…而是…" / "not X, but rather Y" constructions, check for mechanical transition chains, verify sentence length variation, confirm no paragraph ends with a restatement of its opening

## Citation Format

The article must use **numbered footnotes** with full source attributions, NOT internal material IDs. This is a publication-ready article — readers should see proper citations, not internal pipeline references.

**How to convert material IDs to footnotes**:

1. The outline and commentary points reference materials by internal IDs (e.g., M-A-007, Q1-027). These are pointers to excerpt files.
2. Each excerpt has a `Source: SRC-xxx` pointer. Look up the corresponding SRC file in `materials/` to find the source URL, title, and publication.
3. Convert each citation to a Markdown footnote in this format:

```markdown
In the article text, use numbered references like this.[^1] Multiple facts from different sources get separate footnotes.[^2]

[^1]: "Article Title," *Publication Name*, Date. URL
[^2]: Author Name, "Article Title," *Publication Name*, Date. URL
```

**Rules**:
- Number footnotes sequentially throughout the article (starting from `[^1]`)
- If the same source is cited multiple times, reuse the same footnote number
- Include the actual URL from the SRC file so readers can verify the source
- For expert quotes from interview transcripts (Irvin Jim, Xiong Jie), cite as: `Expert Name, interview with the author, Date.` — no URL needed
- Place all footnote definitions at the end of the article
- Do NOT leave any internal IDs (M-A-xxx, Q1-xxx, SRC-xxx) in the article text

## Important Notes

- **Write for humans, not for AI**: This article will be read by the human editor and potentially published. Write accordingly. Actively fight AI writing habits — reread every sentence and ask: would a human journalist write this, or does it sound like a language model?
- **Every sentence must carry substance**: Do not pad paragraphs with filler, setup phrases, or restatements. If a sentence can be deleted without losing information, delete it.
- **Evidence first, always**: If you cannot trace a factual claim to a specific source with a URL, do not make the claim. It is better to have a shorter, well-sourced article than a longer one with unsupported assertions.
- **Do not fabricate**: Do not invent quotes, statistics, or sources. Only use information that exists in the research materials and expert insights.
- **The outline is a guide, not a cage**: If you discover during writing that a different order or emphasis serves the argument better, make a note in the metadata. But generally, follow the outline — it was designed and approved for a reason.
- **The article is a starting point**: Position the output as an excellent first draft, not a finished piece. The human editor will refine it.
- **Style guide is authoritative**: The style guide is a detailed, corpus-derived writing manual. Read it thoroughly and apply its guidance on voice, stance, structure, rhetoric, and language patterns.
