## [M-B-002] Record U.S. Goods Trade Deficit of $1.24 Trillion in 2025
**Source**: SRC-032
**Relevance**: Headline figure showing the U.S. goods trade deficit reached a record $1.24 trillion in 2025, with key breakdowns by trading partner and services surplus data.

> The United States posted a record 1.24 trillion U.S. dollar trade deficit in goods in 2025, up 2.1 percent from the previous year, according to data released Thursday by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis.
>
> Statistics show that the United States' annual exports and imports of goods stood at 2.19 trillion dollars and 3.43 trillion dollars in 2025.
>
> Among main trading partners, the U.S. trade deficit in goods with the European Union dropped by 17.12 billion dollars year on year, while the U.S. trade deficit with Mexico and Vietnam increased by 25.42 billion dollars and 54.73 billion dollars year on year, respectively.
>
> The United States had 339.47 billion dollars of trade surplus in services in 2025, up 27.6 billion dollars or 8.85 percent from the previous year.

## [M-B-004] U.S. Effective Tariff Rate at Historic Highs
**Source**: SRC-031
**Relevance**: Key tariff rate statistics showing the highest effective tariff rates since 1947, with detailed breakdowns before and after the Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs.

> The average effective tariff rate, which reflects actual tariff revenue raised as a share of actual goods imports, was 7.7 percent in 2025, the highest rate since 1947. If the 10 percent Section 122 tariffs expire after 150 days, we estimate the average effective tariff rate will be 5.6 percent in 2026 — the highest since 1972.
>
> The weighted average applied tariff was 1.5 percent in 2022. Prior to the IEEPA ruling, US imports faced a weighted-average applied tariff rate of 13.8 percent. While the 10 percent Section 122 tariffs are in effect, the applied rate will be 10.2 percent, falling to 6.7 percent after they expire.
>
> In calendar year 2025, customs duties raised $264 billion for the federal government, compared to $79 billion in calendar year 2024.

## [M-B-007] Tariff Passthrough to Consumer Prices
**Source**: SRC-033
**Relevance**: Empirical data on how much of tariff costs are passed through to consumers — a crucial indicator of who bears the economic burden.

> The 2025 tariffs have raised an estimated $194.8 billion in inflation-adjusted customs revenue above the 2022-2024 average as of January 2026, with the effective tariff rate reaching 9.9% in December 2025.
>
> During 2025 through December, PCE core goods prices rose 2.0%, versus -0.0% over the same 12-month period starting in 2023. PCE durable goods prices rose 2.1%, versus -2.2% over the same period starting in 2023. Core goods and durable goods were 3.0% and 3.5% above pre-2025 trend in December.
>
> Using the preferred LP trend method, the implied passthrough of tariffs to imported core goods prices through December 2025 is 76%, and the implied passthrough to imported durables prices is 106%.
>
> No evidence of a reduction in non-petroleum import prices as a response to tariffs was found, suggesting foreign producers are not absorbing the cost.

## [M-B-008] Trade Deficit Failed to Shrink Despite Tariffs
**Source**: SRC-032
**Relevance**: Data showing tariffs have not achieved their stated goal of reducing the trade deficit, with expert analysis.

> "The deficit in 2025 shows how little effect tariffs have had, for now, on the overall level of the deficit while distorting monthly trade flows as American businesses adapted to the changes in tariffs," Eugenio Aleman, chief economist at Raymond James, was quoted by MarketWatch as saying.
>
> The tariffs significantly disrupted trade and the effective tariff rate in the United States reached 7.7 percent for 2025, the highest rate since 1947, noted Erica York, vice president of federal tax policy at the Tax Foundation.
>
> "For all the disruption in timing and pattern, the overall balance of trade didn't fundamentally change. We shouldn't expect it to going forward either," York said.

## [M-B-009] Five-Year Trade Deficit Trends: Taiwan, Vietnam, and China
**Source**: SRC-034
**Relevance**: Five-year trend data showing dramatic shifts in bilateral trade patterns, particularly the surge in Taiwan and Vietnam deficits and the decline in China's deficit.

> For the fifth straight year, the U.S. merchandise trade deficit with the world topped $1 trillion.
>
> The United States' single-largest increased deficit from 2020 was with Taiwan, growing from $30.22 billion to $116.54 billion in just five years, an increase of 385.70%. Just the increase in that deficit is greater than the total trade of all but 15 countries in 2025.
>
> The increase in the U.S. deficit with Vietnam was second in size only to Taiwan. It increased $108.52 billion to $178.18 billion, an increase equal to 155.76%, in the same five-year period. U.S. imports of computers, cell phones and computer parts from Vietnam all increased more than 100% from 2024.
>
> The U.S. deficit with China, hobbled by U.S. tariffs since 2018, dropped $105.90 billion, a 34.39% decrease, to $202.07 billion. Five times the size of the U.S. deficit with Mexico in 2018, the U.S. deficit with China was barely larger than the U.S. deficit with Mexico in 2025.
>
> The U.S. deficit with Ireland more than doubled in five years, increasing $58.82 billion to $114.20 billion. Some 96% of the broad hormone and steroid category came from Ireland in 2025.

## [M-B-010] Import Frontloading and Post-Tariff Decline
**Source**: SRC-033
**Relevance**: Data showing the frontloading of imports ahead of tariffs and subsequent decline, illustrating behavioral distortions in trade flows.

> Real imports grew 17.8% over the pre-2025 trend between December 2024 and March 2025 ($50.4 billion in real 2025 dollars) as consumers and businesses made purchases ahead of tariffs. Starting in April, imports declined, and as of December 2025 imports have been on average -6.2% below the pre-2025 trend. Real exports are 2.1% below trend as of December 2025.

## [M-B-016] IEEPA Revenue Potentially Subject to Refund
**Source**: SRC-033
**Relevance**: Estimate of the IEEPA tariff revenue that may need to be refunded following the Supreme Court ruling -- a major fiscal data point.

> On February 20th, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that the use of IEEPA to impose tariffs exceeded presidential authority. Our back-of-the-envelope estimate puts total IEEPA revenue at roughly $168 billion through February 19th, 2026.
>
> Before the 2025 tariff announcements, the average effective US tariff rate was 2.7% (2022-2024 average), raising $7.6 billion a month in net customs duty revenues. The new 2025 tariffs have pushed the average effective US rate to 9.9%, and have raised roughly $194.8 billion in revenues so far, with $174.7 billion collected over the course of 2025 and about $20.1 billion of that new tariff revenue posted in January 2026 alone.
