**BRICS Rare Earths Dominate Global Supply**

BRICS rare earths have become the cornerstone of the global critical minerals market. For instance, this dominance shapes energy transition technologies and mineral processing worldwide. Moreover, BRICS+ countries now hold about 72% of the world's rare earth element (REE) reserves. Consequently, this makes the bloc an essential player in global supply chains.

**Strategic Reserves and Critical Minerals Leadership**

BRICS rare earths are mainly found in China, Brazil, India, Russia, and South Africa. Specifically, China leads with 44 million metric tons of rare earth oxide reserves. Meanwhile, Brazil follows with 21 million metric tons. In addition, India holds nearly 7 million metric tons. Similarly, Russia has almost 4 million metric tons. Therefore, this concentration highlights the critical minerals strength of BRICS+ compared to non-BRICS countries like Australia and the United States.

Furthermore, the total estimated reserves within BRICS+ reach about 75.7 million metric tons. As a result, this represents nearly three-quarters of identified global rare earth resources. These critical minerals support technologies from electric vehicle motors to wind turbines and advanced electronics. Thus, BRICS rare earths define not only mineral wealth but also global technological advancement and energy security.

**Country Breakdown**

- China: Dominates with nearly 90% of global REE refining capacity, processing 270,000 metric tons of REE oxides in 2024 (70% of world mine output) into separated oxides, metals, and magnets; leads in heavy REEs like dysprosium.
- Russia: Significant processing for light REEs, producing ~2,500 MT REE oxides annually; exports refined products to EU (28% of EU REE imports) but lags in magnet production scale.
- India: Emerging capacity at ~2,900 MT REE oxide production/refining, focused on domestic monazite sands; government pushes for separation plants but remains small globally (~1-2%).
- Brazil: Minimal current refining (projects like Serra Verde targeting 5,000 MT/year by 2026); relies on exports of concentrates, with plans for domestic processing tied to graphite/REE synergies.
- South Africa: Limited REE-specific refining; strengths in platinum-group metals processing, with exploratory REE projects but no major oxide separation capacity reported.
- Other BRICS+ (e.g., Iran, UAE, Egypt, Ethiopia): Negligible REE refining; focus on energy/minerals investment rather than operational capacity.

**Mineral Processing: BRICS Rare Earths' Unmatched Refining Power**

China drives mineral processing within BRICS rare earths. In fact, it handles 85-95% of global refining capacity. For example, in 2024 alone, China produced nearly 270,000 metric tons of rare earth oxides. Accordingly, this transforms raw reserves into usable materials for advanced products. Other BRICS nations contribute smaller shares. Specifically, India and Russia produce thousands of metric tons each. Meanwhile, Brazil is expanding projects like Serra Verde. Additionally, South Africa focuses more on platinum-group metals. Collectively, these efforts enhance the broader critical minerals ecosystem.

This mineral processing dominance by BRICS rare earths creates a near-monopolistic position. Therefore, the rest of the world remains heavily dependent on this bloc. Despite global efforts to build processing capabilities elsewhere, BRICS+ expertise and infrastructure remain unmatched.

**Historical Growth and Future Trajectory of BRICS Critical Minerals**

Since 2010, BRICS rare earths have steadily expanded their share of global reserves and production. For instance, China's early export restrictions on rare earths spurred global diversification. However, the bloc's overall dominance stayed strong. Moreover, Brazil and India have advanced exploration projects, reinforcing their positions. The expansion to BRICS+ brought in new members. As a result, they boosted oil, uranium, and mineral investments. Consequently, this has pushed the bloc's clout toward 72-75% of global reserves by the mid-2020s. At the same time, mineral processing shares stayed above 85%.

Trends show modest gains by non-BRICS producers. Nevertheless, reserves and refining remain solidly within BRICS+.

**Strategic Implications: Energy Transition at a Crossroads**

As the world races toward net-zero goals, BRICS+ minerals shape the critical minerals discourse. In particular, their control spans reserves, production, and refining. This fuels debates on supply security. The bloc's assets—China's processing prowess, Brazil's untapped deposits, South Africa's platinum group metals—enable internal trade stability. For example, Russian agencies highlight these as strategic advantages.

Western strategies include stockpiles, recycling initiatives, and trade deals with countries like Australia and Vietnam. However, the European Union still imports heavily from China (46%) and Russia (28%). New projects like Brazil's Serra Verde, aiming for 5,000 metric tons annually by 2026, will likely strengthen BRICS+ control. Ultimately, this could reshape geopolitics around the essential minerals of the energy transition.
