Movement for Historical Reparations for Slavery Gains Momentum in Brazil and Around the World

By Rafael Oliveira, November 21, 2024

[English summary]:

On November 19, 1993, a group of 12 black activists staged a protest at the Maksoud Plaza, a five-star hotel in São Paulo. When the bill arrived, the protesters said the amounts could be "hung" on the country's historic debt to the Black population. The event marked the launch of the Movement for Reparations for Afro-descendants (MPR), which demanded that the Brazilian state pay compensation to each of the descendants of enslaved people in the country. The total amount demanded exceeded $6 trillion at the time.

More than 30 years later, on November 21, 2024, the Brazilian federal government (União) formally apologized for the enslavement of people in an event organized by the Advocacia-Geral da União (AGU), the Ministry of Racial Equality and the organization Educafro Brasil. "A União manifesta publicamente seu pedido de desculpas pela escravização das pessoas negras, bem como de seus efeitos," said the advocate-general Jorge Messias.

Brazil is, by far, the country that received the largest contingent of enslaved Africans. According to the Slave Voyages Database, 4.86 million enslaved people were disembarked in Brazilian territory between 1501 and 1900. According to a study by the University of West Indies, in partnership with the American Society of International Laws, the reparations value for slavery in Brazil could reach R$135 trillion.

More than 130 years after abolition, the Black movement has won important advances in reparatory justice: the Racial Equality Statute, racial quotas in universities and public service (in 2020, 52% of students in federal universities were Black or Brown, up from 41% in 2010), and quilombola land titling. However, these achievements are considered insufficient to repair the legacy of inequality caused by centuries of slavery.

Under President Lula, a Coordination-General for Memory and Truth of Slavery and the Transatlantic Trafficking of Enslaved Persons was created under the Ministry of Human Rights and Citizenship. Researcher Fernanda Thomaz states that "the population that suffered from slavery is the same one that today has the worst conditions of housing, health, access to education, access to work, and suffers from enormous police violence."

An unprecedented civil inquiry is targeting Banco do Brasil (BB), Brazil's oldest bank. Historians found that much of the capital used in the bank's refoundation in 1853 came from the slave trade, which was already prohibited at that time. The BB issued a public apology and announced a "pact for racial equality" in partnership with the Ministry of Racial Equality.

At the UN Permanent Forum on People of African Descent in April 2025, Brazil's Minister of Racial Equality Anielle Franco stated that slavery was a crime against humanity and "must be faced with courage."

Colombia's Vice President Francia Marquez also called for a global reparations fund at the same forum, saying: "Colonial states... have to assume the responsibility of restoring the human dignity of people of African descent."
