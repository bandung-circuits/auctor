Brazilian political party seeks to block US$2.8bn acquisition of rare earth firm by USA Rare Earth
Bnamericas Published: Tuesday, April 28, 2026

The Brazilian political party Rede Sustentabilidade will try to block the sale of control of the rare earth mining company Serra Verde to USA Rare Earth (USAR), adding political risks to a sector that is increasingly in the spotlight.

Recently, USA Rare Earth (USAR) reached an agreement to acquire control of Serra Verde for a total amount of US$2.8 billion (bn). With the transaction, the company will come to hold 100% of Serra Verde, owner of the Pela Ema rare earth mine and processing plant in the state of Goiás.

Now, the political party is seeking to overturn the agreement through a lawsuit in the Federal Supreme Court, STF.

"For Rede, this type of arrangement can, in practice, shift the decision-making power over the exploitation of rare earths – a strategic resource of the Union – to a foreign group, without prior and in-depth analysis by the State of the impacts on the national interest. The party points out that Article 176 of the Constitution establishes that mineral resources belong to the Union and that exploitation must take place in the national interest, by means of authorization or concession, which would require more effective control by the State", the STF said in a statement.

"In the action, the party requests an injunction so that the Union and the National Mining Agency (ANM) present documents, opinions, and analyses on the questioned corporate operations. It also asks the regulatory body to detail the assessment of the national interest, technological impacts, and economic sovereignty, and that the related acts be suspended if the justification is insufficient. On the merits, it requests the recognition of flaws in the current model and the adoption of more stringent control mechanisms," he emphasized.

The STF did not set an exact deadline to evaluate the issue.

The Rede party was founded by Brazil's current minister of the environment, Marina Silva, and is part of the congressional coalition for the federal government.

Some specialists see the chances of reversing the agreement as low, since Serra Verde is controlled by the private equity funds Denham Capital and EMG in the United States, and Vision Blue in the United Kingdom.

However, given the growing political elements involving the sector, future agreements involving the transfer of control of companies and rare earth projects in Brazil may face more restrictions.

"Mineral resources belong to the Union and to the Brazilian people. This means that negotiations of this kind end up pressuring the federal government to react, which can lead to more interventionist proposals, such as the creation of a state-owned [company] – something I consider a step backwards", said Marisa Cesar, chair of the board of the Critical Minerals Association (AMC), to BNamericas.

Brazil holds the second-largest volume of rare earth reserves in the world, behind only China, and the country is seen as an alternative for the United States and others to diversify their sources of these inputs, which are essential for the defense, technology, and energy sectors.

The issue has definitively entered the political debate. The current left-wing president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, has emphasized that Brazil is seeking to attract multiple partners for its rare earth projects, but wants to avoid having the processing of these minerals carried out outside the country, in an attempt to create a local beneficiation chain.

Lula's narrative is also a way of opposing the current president of the United States, Donald Trump, who has adopted an aggressive policy against international trade partners. Lula has used this strategy amid the approach of the presidential elections in Brazil, which will take place in October, and has already said that he intends to run.

On the other hand, one of the right-wing pre-candidates, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, said that he is against any kind of restriction.

"Are rare earths of interest to the world? Come here, we're going to transfer technology here. Brazil exports a shipload of iron ore to China and gets back a canoe of laptops? Why don't we add value to this product here?" said Flávio Bolsonaro in a video on his social network.

The approach of the electoral campaign in Brazil, combined with the growing geopolitical dispute between the United States and China, has definitively put Brazilian rare earths on the global map.
