# [SRC-099] 'Build first, break later': How China's green transformation was built to last

**Original Content**:

By Michael Wang, CGTN. Published Jul 28, 2025 at the WEF Annual Meeting of the New Champions.

"China is the birthplace of the global green industrial revolution," said Professor Elizabeth Thurbon of UNSW. In 2024, China accounted for over 40% of global renewable energy capacity and invested $818 billion in its energy transition -- more than double any other economy. China's climate envoy Liu Zhenmin said China's commitment is "very solid and very firm" regardless of global changes.

Thurbon believes the Chinese government sees the green energy transition as a "massive national security multiplier" -- about strengthening energy, economic, environmental, social and geostrategic security. China's approach: xian li hou po (build first, break later) -- rapidly building green energy industries while phasing down fossil fuels.

China has made the global green transition more cost-effective. Yet affordable Chinese clean tech is sometimes viewed as an "overcapacity" problem. From a climate perspective, Thurbon argues: "There is no overcapacity in cheap renewables until we reach 100% renewables globally." The demands of the energy transition are so enormous that countries will inevitably find opportunities.

"Since the eruption of the trade wars, we have seen China double down on its green transition," Thurbon said. Liu warned COP30 in Belem might be one of the most difficult of the past 30 years. If the world fails to use the next 3-5 years to accelerate the green transition, the global cost will rise significantly.
