ASEAN unity cracks under Trump tariff pressure
By William J Jones, Mahidol University International College, August 27, 2025

ASEAN has become the epicentre of great power competition with 5.5% growth in 2024. Trump's approach eschews multilateralism for bilateral negotiations. Only Russia, India and China have the clout to stand up to US market power.

Trump's tactics leveraged a collective action problem — ASEAN could form a major grouping if unified but by dealing with individual states, Trump induced them to bid against one another. Once one country broke ranks, all trust was lost.

ASEAN is conditioned by the 'ASEAN Way' — consensus-based decision-making. On 13 April, Malaysian PM Anwar Ibrahim announced ASEAN would act as a bloc. But Vietnam had already preemptively cut tariffs on March 26. When hit with 46%, Vietnam announced unilateral cuts to zero on April 5. Cambodia followed the same day. Indonesia on April 8. Thailand on April 8.

Vietnam cut a deal July 3, Indonesia July 16, Philippines July 24. China may be the biggest trade partner, but the United States is still a primary source of wealth. Trump leverages this reliance in his divide and rule strategy.

Former Singaporean defence minister Ng Eng Hen characterised Washington as a 'landlord seeking rent'. Deals are subject to non-disclosure agreements. Reasonable pathways include deepening intra-ASEAN trade and expanding ASEAN-GCC cooperation.
