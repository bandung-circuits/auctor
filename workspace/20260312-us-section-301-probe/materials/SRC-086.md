India-U.S. interim trade deal triggers farmer concern
Mongabay India, 9 Feb 2026

On February 6, the U.S. and India announced a framework for an interim reciprocal trade agreement. The announcement raised concern among farmer groups and food security activists over potential increases in agricultural imports, including genetically modified (GM) grain.

India will eliminate or reduce tariffs on U.S. industrial goods and a wide range of food and agricultural products, including dried distillers' grains (DDGs), red sorghum for animal feed, tree nuts, fresh and processed fruit, soybean oil, wine and spirits. India also agreed to address non-tariff barriers affecting U.S. food and agricultural exports.

Farmer organisations called for nationwide protests on February 12. ASHA-Kisan Swaraj warned that removal of tariffs could depress domestic prices, affect farmers, distort feed and livestock markets, and increase dependence on imports. Concerns were also raised about potential imports of GM food products, particularly soybean oil and DDGs from GM maize.

The US will apply an 18% reciprocal tariff on Indian products. India intends to purchase $500 billion worth of U.S. energy products, aircraft, precious metals, technology products, and coking coal over the next five years.
