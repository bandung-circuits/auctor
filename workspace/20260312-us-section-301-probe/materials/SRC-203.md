China Pauses Some Rare Earth Export Curbs While Retaining Levers of Control
Policy Brief by Jack Burnham and Duncan Lazarow, November 12, 2025

On November 9, China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) announced a one-year delay in the implementation of certain rare earth export controls following a recent meeting between Xi Jinping and President Donald Trump in Busan, South Korea. China's decision to delay the new measures, rather than canceling them, showcases its desire to maintain its options.

MOFCOM paused the December 2024 ban on gallium, germanium, antimony exports. Beijing also introduced a one-year delay on October 2025 controls limiting export of products containing more than 0.1 percent rare earths from China.

However, Beijing maintained the April 4, 2025 rare earth licensing system requiring case-by-case permission to export seven rare earth elements and permanent magnet materials, effectively cutting off flow to Western military manufacturers.

Control timeline:
- December 2024: Gallium, germanium, antimony — Paused 1 year
- April 2025: 7 rare earth elements and permanent magnets — Active
- October 2025: Expanded rare earths plus processing technology — Paused 1 year

China has spent three decades consolidating dominance: roughly 89 percent of global rare-earth refining. China's enforcement bureaucracy is expanding at its fastest pace since 2022.

Washington should approach the pause as a tactical opening to cement ally-shoring deals, not an enduring concession.
