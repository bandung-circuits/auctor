South Korean autoworkers face attacks on working conditions from Trump tariffs
By Ben McGrath, 9 July 2025

South Korea's auto industry faces growing opposition from the working class in the face of Trump's trade tariffs. Workers at GM Korea voted to strike, with 88.2% of 6,851 union members supporting a walkout — the highest in the union's history.

Washington imposed a 25% tariff on vehicles and auto parts from South Korea. Trump announced a 25% "reciprocal" tariff on other South Korean imports as well, effective August 1. South Korea's economy is expected to grow by only 0.8% in 2025; without a trade deal, the economy could decline by 0.4%.

Auto companies are exploiting the Trump tariffs to accelerate attacks on jobs, pay and conditions. GM Korea exports approximately 410,000 vehicles annually, with 85% going to the US. Despite an operating profit of 1.36 trillion won ($990 million) in 2024, GM Korea has rejected wage requests, claiming expected decline in sales due to tariffs.

GM Korea has already carried out plant closures in recent years, including the Gunsan plant in 2018 and suspension of operations at Bupyeong Plant 2 in 2022, despite receiving 810 billion won ($590 million) in public money in 2018 to maintain production.
