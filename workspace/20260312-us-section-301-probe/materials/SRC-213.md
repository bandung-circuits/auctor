Vietnam manufacturing is caught in U.S.-China trade war

By Jane Seidel, April 18, 2025

Trump slapped a 46% levy on imports from Vietnam, which could prove disastrous for Vietnam, home to factories of Samsung and Intel and suppliers of Apple.

Vietnam's transformation into a global tech manufacturing hub started in 2008 when Samsung broke ground for its first smartphone factory. In 2018, when Trump launched a trade war against China, Vietnam emerged as a breakout star of the "China-plus-one" strategy.

Today, most Samsung smartphones are made in Vietnam. Apple has moved production of iPads, MacBooks, AirPods, and Apple Watches there.

By mid-2023, a global demand slump hit Vietnam's assembly lines hard. More than 45,000 workers lost jobs in the first five months of 2023, revealing how fragile the hyperconnected tech supply chains are.

With factory jobs drying up, some women workers pivoted to Vietnam's beauty industry.

As the industry recovered, manufacturers faced a new challenge: winning back workers they had previously laid off. Factories raised wages and offered record-high bonuses, free housing, and gift packages.

"The reciprocal tariff on China still complicates the issue for Vietnam as China is Vietnam's leading trade partner and provides parts and components for Vietnam's manufacturing sector," said Hanh Nguyen, Australian National University.
