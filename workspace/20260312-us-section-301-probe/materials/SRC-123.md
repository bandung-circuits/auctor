China's Overcapacity and the Global South: An Opportunity for the EU. By Aya Adachi, March 26, 2025.

China's 14th Five-Year Plan speaks of a strategic commitment to maintaining large-scale production in low-tech sectors, contradicting expectations that developing nations would take over these industries.

For Global South economies, China's continued dominance in low-value industries undercuts domestic production in developing countries, stifles local industry growth, and reinforces trade imbalances.

Deepening Trade Imbalances: In upper-middle-income nations (Brazil, South Africa), China has transitioned from net importer of machinery to establishing a dominant surplus. In lower-middle-income countries (India, Indonesia, Nigeria), China's exports have surged.

The Global South Is Pushing Back: India, Brazil, Argentina have implemented tariffs, anti-dumping duties, and local content requirements. Unlike Western states, Global South countries have not faced significant retaliation from Beijing.

Some countries are adopting measures to encourage Chinese investment with local value-added processing. Turkey, Brazil, Indonesia, Zimbabwe attract Chinese capital under conditions requiring technology transfer and local sourcing. Even Benin pushes for agro-processing investment.

Many are looking to the EU for support in strengthening trade resilience.
