Wanted in Budget 2026: A Trump shock absorber for the small enterprises to survive

January 2026

As Budget 2026 approaches, a sector accounting for nearly half of India's exports has had anything but a smooth year. Donald Trump's tariff tantrums have rattled MSMEs -- the micro, small and medium enterprises that form the backbone of India's trade.

The sector contributes nearly 30% to India's GDP, sits at the heart of the country's growth and jobs engine, and is set to be hit hard by the US' hardliner trade tactics.

US President Donald Trump slapped tariffs of 50% on Indian goods in August, one of the highest rates in the world, slashing exports to India's biggest market and threatening PM Modi's manufacturing ambitions.

The imposition of higher tariffs by the US is set to significantly impact the micro, small and medium enterprise sector, which accounts for around 45% of India's exports, while MSMEs in textiles, diamonds and chemicals are likely to be the most hit, a report by CRISIL Intelligence had said.

The MSMEs have more than 70% share in sectors such as textiles, gems and jewellery and will be hit hard. Another sector which is likely to face the heat is chemicals, where MSMEs have a 40% share. Diamonds account for over 50% of the country's gems and jewellery exports.

At present, MSMEs face a credit gap of as much as Rs 30 lakh crore, according to SIDBI.

"MSMEs face persistent challenges such as limited access to finance, knowledge gaps, scalability issues and slow adoption of quality standards," said Vineeta Hariharan, Advisor, Public Policy at Deloitte India.
