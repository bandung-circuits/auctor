> Canada, which is the US's second largest trading partner, was not mentioned as a target of the probe.

**Relevance**: NBC News reporting includes a remarkable anecdote about Trump setting Switzerland's tariff rate at 39% based on personal irritation with the Swiss president, revealing how individual caprice rather than trade analysis can drive tariff decisions -- an angle largely absent from non-US media coverage.

>
> Describing Switzerland's then-president Karin Keller-Sutter, Trump said she, "kept saying the same thing over and over, 'we are a small country.'"
>
> "And she just rubbed me the wrong way, I'll be honest with you," Trump recounted. He said that after hanging up, he decided the tariff on Switzerland would be 39%.
>
> One top member of the European Parliament said the Supreme Court ruling — paired with Trump's new 10% duties — had created "pure tariff chaos."
>

## [M-G-006] Channel News Asia: Southeast Asian analysts warn Section 301 could target supply chain diversification itself
**Source**: SRC-045
**Relevance**: Singapore-based Channel News Asia provides expert analysis from ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute fellows warning that the Section 301 probes could target precisely the kind of supply chain diversification Southeast Asian countries built in response to earlier US-China tensions -- a cruel irony that reveals the contradictions in US trade strategy.

> "The fundamental direction of US trade policy hasn't changed," said Dr Jayant Menon, senior fellow at the ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute. "The administration remains committed to using tariffs as leverage. What's shifted is the legal pathway, not the intent."
>
> "The concern is that Section 301 could be used to target precisely the kind of supply chain diversification that these countries have built," said Cassey Lee, senior fellow at ISEAS. "If the US views Southeast Asian manufacturing growth as an extension of Chinese overcapacity, it could apply tariffs that would undermine the region's competitive advantage."
>
> "Countries that rushed to sign deals may find themselves in a worse position," said Dr Menon. "They made concessions — on procurement, on digital trade rules — that may not be reciprocated if the tariff framework changes."

## [M-G-007] Sourcing Journal (trade industry): Insider analysis revealing investigations are "checking the boxes"
**Source**: SRC-054
**Relevance**: The trade industry publication provides the most critical insider perspective, with industry representatives explicitly stating the investigations are less about fact-finding and more about establishing a legal basis for predetermined tariff outcomes. It also notes the strategic timing vis-a-vis China and EU negotiations.

> "With IEEPA being taken away, they've lost a lot of leverage, and they're trying to regenerate that," Matt Priest, CEO and president of the Footwear Distributors and Retailers of America (FDRA), said of the Trump administration going on the Section 301 offensive.
>
> The trade association lead noted that the timing of investigations into certain economies is no coincidence. Turning up the heat on China weeks ahead of a meeting in Beijing between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping and targeting the EU, which recently put its reciprocal trade agreement with the U.S. "on ice," are almost certainly calculated attempts to exert control over negotiations.
>
> With the administration's goal of reprising the IEEPA duties all but explicitly stated, Priest believes the investigations will be less about fact-finding and more about seeking confirmation.
>
> "I think a lot of the 'investigative' part in their minds is probably just checking the boxes on process; a lot of their concerns have been well documented," he said.
>
> "I think they know what they want to go after," Priest said. "I don't think there's going to be a whole lot of Sherlock Holmes activity."

## [M-G-008] Economic Times India: Explainer format contextualizing the probe for Indian audiences
**Source**: SRC-056
**Relevance**: Indian media adopts an explainer format that contextualizes the probe for domestic audiences, emphasizing India's specific vulnerabilities and the history of Section 301's contested legality at the WTO.

> For India, inclusion in the new investigation signals renewed scrutiny of its industrial and trade policies by Washington.
>
> If the probe concludes that certain practices distort trade, the US could impose tariffs or other restrictions on Indian exports to the American market. The United States remains one of India's largest trading partners, meaning any punitive measures could have implications for sectors such as manufacturing and exports.
>
> At the same time, Section 301 probes often lead to negotiations, meaning countries under scrutiny may seek diplomatic or policy solutions before tariffs are imposed.
>
> In 1998, the European Union challenged the provision at the WTO, arguing that unilateral trade sanctions could bypass the global dispute settlement system. Countries including India, Brazil, China, Japan and Canada joined the case as third parties.

## [M-G-011] EU Parliament trade committee chair: "Deal is a deal" but US has breached it
**Source**: SRC-063
**Relevance**: European media coverage reveals significant EU frustration, with the European Parliament's trade committee chair explicitly accusing the US of breaching the trade deal multiple times and putting the Anti-Coercion Instrument on the table as a retaliatory option.

> "We wanted to have really stability and predictability. And unfortunately, the government, the president of the United States, has really made a breach of this deal several times," Bernd Lange, chair of the European Parliament's international trade committee, told CNBC.
>
> "We are sticking to the deal. A deal is a deal, no doubt about. But on the U.S. side, there was a breaking [of the agreement] some weeks after the deal was concluded, 400 products — so-called derivatives — were lifted from 15 to 50% and this is really harming a lot of small and medium-sized enterprises in Europe."
>

## [M-G-012] Lowy Institute analysis: Southeast Asia's paradoxical position
**Source**: SRC-044
**Relevance**: The Lowy Institute highlights the ironic position of Southeast Asian countries that benefited from trade diversion under Trump's China tariffs, only to now face scrutiny themselves under the Section 301 probes.

> Southeast Asian countries were among those most heavily targeted by Donald Trump's so-called reciprocal tariffs – now struck down by the US Supreme Court. Paradoxically, however, the region was benefitting handsomely from Trump's tariffs via trade diversion and the shifting of supply chains out of China.
>
> If so, then it's a cruel irony for these and other countries that, under duress, rushed to do quick tariff-reducing deals with the Trump administration.
>
> The key variable in all this is potential new tariffs based on Section 301 investigations into whether individual countries are treating US firms or interests unfairly.
>
> Taking a longer view however, any pause in economic tensions between the US and China will remain extremely fragile. Geopolitical risk is therefore likely to remain a powerful force supporting the shift of supply chains out of China and into Southeast Asia. But if China does end up facing lower US tariffs than Southeast Asia, even if only temporarily, it could slow that shift down considerably.
