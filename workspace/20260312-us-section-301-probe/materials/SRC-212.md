Hit By US Tariff Escalation, Indian Agriculture In Need Of Complete Overhaul

By Avanindra Nath Thakur (Professor of Economics, Jindal School Of Government And Public Policy) and Amartya Jha

December 2025

The impact of the US tariff escalation on agricultural goods is expected to be significant as the US is one of the largest importers of Indian agricultural products in FY25 (US$ 5.62 billion), accounting for 10.98 per cent of total Indian exports. Key exports include seafood (primarily frozen shrimp), spices, basmati rice, processed fruits and vegetables. These are directly linked to the livelihood of Indian farmers.

India's agricultural sector contributes 16 per cent of GDP while providing employment to 46 per cent of the population.

In early December 2025, Trump hinted at new tariffs on rice imports from India. The US has used Indian agriculture as both a target and a bargaining chip.

The US, Argentina, Canada, Ukraine and Australia have legally challenged India's market price support for rice and wheat at the WTO, stating it is around 87 per cent of production value, well above the WTO's 10 per cent limit.

Small and marginal farmers face highly uneven and inequitable access to credit, input markets, and procurement. Granting market access would have long term and irreversible negative consequences for employment, food security and price stability.

Indian agriculture needs a complete overhaul: diversify export destinations, shift from raw goods to processed goods, and build a comprehensive support system for small and marginal farmers.
