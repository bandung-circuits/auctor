# [SRC-Q6-005] Resource Nationalism in Critical Minerals: DRC, Indonesia, Chile, and Others

**Source URL**: https://criticalstrategicmetals.com/geopolitics/resource-nationalism/
**Fetch Time**: 2026-05-15T15:20:00Z
**Source Type**: Industry Reports
**Credibility**: Medium-High

**Original Content**:

Resource nationalism, the assertion of greater state control over natural resources to capture a larger share of economic value, is reshaping the global critical minerals landscape. Mineral-rich nations in Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia are no longer content to serve as raw material exporters while the lucrative processing, manufacturing, and technology stages of the value chain occur elsewhere. Instead, governments are deploying a widening arsenal of policy tools - export bans on unprocessed ores, mandatory domestic processing requirements, increased royalty rates, renegotiation of mining contracts, and outright nationalization of mineral assets - to assert sovereignty over their geological endowments.

The energy transition has dramatically increased the strategic value of minerals such as lithium, cobalt, nickel, and rare earths, giving producing nations greater leverage over consuming nations that urgently need these materials. Historical grievances about the "resource curse" - where mineral wealth enriches foreign companies and local elites while broader populations see little benefit - have fueled political demands for change. And the success of early movers, particularly Indonesia's nickel strategy, has provided a template that other nations are eager to replicate.

Indonesia: High intensity. Nickel. Policy since 2020. Status: Active.
Reserves: #1 globally, 52% of global output.
Policy overview: Banned raw nickel ore exports in 2020, forcing over $30bn in downstream investment. Extended to bauxite in 2023. Now the world's dominant nickel producer.
In depth: President Joko Widodo's ore export ban transformed Indonesia from a raw material supplier into the world's largest nickel products exporter. Chinese companies including Tsingshan, Huayou Cobalt, and CATL invested billions in smelters and battery material plants. By 2024 Indonesia produced over half of global nickel output. The WTO ruled against Indonesia in 2022 but the ruling is unenforceable due to a paralysed Appellate Body.

DR Congo: High intensity. Cobalt. Policy since 2018. Status: Ongoing.
Reserves: 50%+ of global reserves, 70%+ of global mine output.
Policy overview: Reclassified cobalt as a "strategic substance" in 2018, raising royalties to 10%. Reviews mining contracts for renegotiation and periodically threatens export suspensions.
In depth: The 2018 Mining Code revision was the DRC's most significant assertion of resource nationalism in decades. Cobalt royalties rose from 2% to 10%. The government of President Tshisekedi launched broad reviews of contracts signed under his predecessor Kabila. State miner Gecamines has pursued larger equity stakes in joint ventures with Chinese and Western partners. China's dominant position across DRC cobalt operations limits how aggressively Kinshasa can push reform.

Chile: Medium intensity. Lithium. Policy since 2023. Status: Transitioning.
Reserves: #1 globally, ~30% of global production.
Policy overview: President Boric announced a National Lithium Strategy requiring majority state control in future projects. A Codelco-SQM partnership was agreed for the Atacama operations.
In depth: Chile's April 2023 announcement marked a historic shift after decades of market-oriented mining policy. Existing SQM and Albemarle contracts were not immediately cancelled, but future projects must partner with the state. In December 2023, state copper company Codelco agreed to take majority control of Atacama operations alongside SQM. The government argues lithium revenues must fund social programmes and that a sovereign wealth model is essential for intergenerational equity.

Zimbabwe: Medium intensity. Lithium. Policy since 2022. Status: Active.
Reserves: Top 5 globally, Emerging producer.
Policy overview: Banned raw lithium ore exports in December 2022. Chinese companies (Sinomine, Huayou Cobalt) have invested in domestic processing to comply.
In depth: Zimbabwe holds significant spodumene deposits at Bikita and Arcadia mines. The export ban requires all lithium to be beneficiated or processed before leaving the country, explicitly modelled on the Indonesian nickel approach. Chinese investors have responded by building processing capacity in-country.

Mexico: High intensity. Lithium. Policy since 2022. Status: Active.
Policy overview: President AMLO nationalized lithium in 2022 via constitutional reform, creating state company LitioMx. Cancelled Ganfeng Lithium's Sonora project permits.
In depth: Mexico's 2022 constitutional amendment declared lithium a property of the nation and prohibited private mining concessions. President AMLO created LitioMx, a state company tasked with all lithium extraction. The move cancelled or suspended major projects, including Bacanora Lithium's Sonora project (backed by China's Ganfeng Lithium). Mexico's approach is the most aggressive lithium nationalization globally.

Namibia: Medium intensity. Lithium / Green Hydrogen. Policy since 2023. Status: Emerging.
Policy overview: Introduced a lithium ore export ban in 2023 following Zimbabwe's model. Part of a broader local beneficiation push across multiple mineral sectors.
