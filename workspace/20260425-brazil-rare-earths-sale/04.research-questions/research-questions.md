### Research Group Q1: Legal Prospects & Strategic Implications of Rede's ADPF
**Priority**: High
**Rationale**: Addresses Expert Questions 1 and 2 (Rede's ADPF prospects and strategic calculus). Also fills REF-MRX-001 Layer 5 gap (State Power) — understanding how the Brazilian state apparatus may respond to constitutional challenge on strategic mineral sovereignty, and what institutional forces shape the Court's likely disposition.

1. What are the procedural pathways and typical timelines for an ADPF (Arguição de Descumprimento de Preceito Fundamental) at Brazil's Supreme Federal Court, from filing to preliminary injunction to final decision?
2. What is the current composition of Brazil's Supreme Court regarding sovereignty, resource governance, and foreign investment rulings — which justices have track records relevant to Rede's constitutional arguments?
3. Has Brazil's Supreme Court previously ruled on foreign control of strategic mineral or natural resources, and what precedents (if any) exist for constitutional protection of national interest in resource exploitation?
4. What is Rede Sustentabilidade's broader strategic agenda — does the party have a documented history of constitutional reform advocacy, and what evidence exists that this ADPF is intended as a catalyst for broader legislative action rather than a case-specific blocking attempt?
5. What positions have other governing coalition parties (PT, PSB, PDT) taken on Rede's ADPF, and what political dynamics within the PT-led coalition does this action reveal regarding the divide between statist and market-oriented wings?

### Research Group Q2: Brazil's Regulatory Architecture & the Critical Minerals Bill
**Priority**: High
**Rationale**: Addresses Expert Question 6 (critical minerals bill details and regulatory gap). Fills REF-MRX-001 Layer 5 (State Power) and REF-MRX-004 gap — understanding how Brazil's regulatory vacuum enables imperialist resource capture, and what institutional reforms are being proposed.

1. What are the specific provisions of the critical minerals bill sent to Congress — does it create a formal foreign investment screening mechanism equivalent to CFIUS (US) or FIRB (Australia), or merely authorize ad hoc government veto power over mining company sales?
2. What is the bill's legislative status, expected timeline for committee consideration and floor vote, and likelihood of passage given current congressional composition and party alignments?
3. Would the bill's provisions apply retroactively to the Serra Verde deal or similar pending transactions, and what constitutional/legal constraints exist on retroactive application of investment screening legislation in Brazil?
4. How do existing Brazilian regulatory bodies (ANM, CADE, Ministry of Mines and Energy) currently evaluate foreign acquisitions of mining companies — what specific authority, criteria, and precedent do they have for national interest determinations?
5. What comparative models exist for critical mineral investment screening in other Global South nations (Indonesia's nickel export controls, Chile's lithium governance framework, Mexico's 2022 mining reform), and what lessons do they offer for Brazil's regulatory gap?

### Research Group Q3: Non-Alignment under Structural Asymmetry & Alternative Development Models
**Priority**: High
**Rationale**: Addresses Expert Questions 3 and 4 (sustainability of non-alignment and Indonesia nickel model lessons). Also addresses key gap on Shenghe Resources/private-sector response and MagBras viability. Maps to REF-MRX-003 Contradictions 3 and 6 (Popular classes vs US-led elite; US imperialism vs sovereign nations) and REF-MRX-004 containment strategy analysis.

1. How does the 15-year exclusive offtake agreement's structure effectively negate Brazil's declared "non-alignment" position — what specific provisions (SPV exclusivity, prohibited third-party sales, floor pricing tied to US strategic requirements) foreclose Brazilian or third-party access to Serra Verde's output?
2. What would a genuinely non-aligned framework for rare earth development look like — are there existing models (Indonesia's nickel strategy with Chinese processing investment domestically, Chile's national lithium strategy) that maintain sovereign control while attracting necessary capital and technical expertise?
3. What were the specific policy steps Indonesia took in its nickel export ban and downstream industrialization strategy (timeline, ban dates, graduated restrictions, incentives for domestic processing), and what institutional and financial prerequisites would Brazil need to replicate such an approach for rare earths?
4. How has Shenghe Resources and other Chinese private-sector REE entities responded to losing Serra Verde's output — have they sought alternative Brazilian suppliers, adjusted pricing strategies, pursued legal or commercial countermeasures, or shifted focus to other ionic clay sources?
5. What is the current status and technical viability of Brazil's MagBras (Magnet Bras) initiative — what path exists from the 20 kg rare earth carbonate demonstration to industrial-scale processing, what is the projected timeline, and what funding and institutional requirements would be needed?

### Research Group Q4: Ionic Clay Deposits & Bargaining Power Dynamics
**Priority**: Medium
**Rationale**: Addresses Expert Question 5 (ionic clay deposit significance for bargaining power). Fills material conditions gap (REF-MRX-001 Layer 2) — understanding whether geological rarity translates to political leverage or whether the processing bottleneck negates it entirely.

1. How many ionic clay REE deposits exist globally outside China and Myanmar, and what is Pela Ema's specific geological uniqueness (heavy rare earth ratios, extraction efficiency, deposit scale) compared to these alternatives?
2. What processing technologies and expertise are required to extract heavy rare earths from ionic clay deposits, and what is the current global distribution of such processing capability — is it concentrated exclusively in China?
3. Does Brazil possess any indigenous ionic clay processing expertise, pilot facilities, or research programs, and what technical and institutional investments would be required to develop domestic processing capacity for Pela Ema-type ores?
4. How has the rarity of ionic clay HREE deposits influenced resource governance negotiations in other countries — have Vietnam (Dong Pao deposit), Thailand, or other nations with similar deposits leveraged geological uniqueness for sovereign benefit or sovereign bargaining?
5. What is the relationship between ionic clay deposit rarity and market pricing dynamics — does geological scarcity translate to higher bargaining power and pricing leverage for the host nation, or does the downstream processing bottleneck negate this advantage entirely, keeping host nations in a raw-material-supplier position?

**Rationale**: Addresses Expert Question 7 (environmental and social dimensions omitted from legal/media framings). Fills identified key gap on labor and community conditions. Maps to REF-MRX-001 Layer 3 (urban-rural divide, national oppression) and ecological dimension (metabolic rift) — rare earth mining's environmental devastation as a core manifestation of the metabolic rift.

1. What environmental monitoring, regulatory oversight, and licensing conditions currently exist at the Pela Ema mine, and what are the documented or projected environmental impacts of ionic clay REE extraction (water contamination, radioactive waste handling, hazardous dust, land degradation)?
2. What quilombola and Indigenous communities exist in the vicinity of the Pela Ema mine in Goias, and what formal protections — consultation rights under ILO Convention 169/CF 1988 Article 231, environmental licensing conditions, community benefit agreements — apply to them under Brazilian law?
3. What are the labor conditions at Serra Verde — workforce composition (local vs imported), wage levels relative to regional averages, unionization status, occupational health and safety protections for rare earth mining, and community economic impact?
4. What environmental and social provisions (if any) exist in the USAR acquisition agreement or DFC loan conditions — does the DFC's Environmental and Social Impact Assessment (ESIA) requirement include specific community benefit or environmental safeguard obligations for Pela Ema?
5. What is the historical environmental legacy of rare earth mining in similar ionic clay operations globally (China's Jiangxi Province contamination, Myanmar's Kachin State mining impacts), and what lessons do these precedents offer for understanding the environmental risks at Pela Ema that Brazilian regulatory framings have omitted?
