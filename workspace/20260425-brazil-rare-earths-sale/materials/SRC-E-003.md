The Relaunching of Industrial Policy in Brazil: What Have Rare Earths Got to Do with It?
Author: Manuel Mindreau
Published: December 2025, The Extractive Industries and Society, Volume 24, Article 101723
DOI: 10.1016/j.exis.2025.101723
Open Access, Creative Commons License

Highlights:
- Brazil has been slow to integrate rare earths into the policy agenda and promote economic upgrading of the sector.
- The scientific community has played a pivotal role in the development of the rare earths sector.
- Brazil lacks a national policy for the strategic management of critical minerals.
- The presence of industrial policy plans favours the strategic management of rare earths.
- In the absence of industrial policy, traditional commodity export schemes have taken precedence regarding critical minerals.

Abstract:
With the purpose of reverting the process of premature 'de-industrialization' suffered by the Brazilian economy in the previous decade, the Lula III administration has embarked on implementing a new industrial programme using a 'mission-driven' design. Several of the missions prioritised by Nova Industria Brasil, officially announced in January 2024, will imply a greater demand for rare earths and other critical minerals necessary both for the energy and digital transitions, and the defence industry -- integral components of the 'neoindustrialisation' objectives contemplated in the missions to be accomplished. Furthermore, in a setting of global great power rivalry and exacerbated supply chain failures, the quest for securing access to critical minerals away from China is offering Brazil, a country with large reserves of rare earths, additional prospects for linking production of these minerals to industrial policy. But how is the Federal Government responding to this opportunity for economic upgrading in its rare earths sector? The question will be examined by comparing Brazil's attempts at strategic management of rare earths over the 2010-2025 period within the context of President Rousseff's Plano Brasil Maior, the absence of industrial policy during the Temer and Bolsonaro administrations, and the recently launched Nova Industria Brasil.
