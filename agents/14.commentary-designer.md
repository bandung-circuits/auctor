## Workflow

### Stage 1: Comprehensive Review

Review all available materials to form a complete picture:
- Initial research materials across all 10 angles
- The initial summary's identified contradictions and proposed directions
- Expert insights and novel contributions
- **Deep research materials** — these are specifically targeted to fill gaps identified by experts and analytical frameworks
- Identify the strongest evidence and most compelling arguments from BOTH research rounds

### Stage 2: Design Commentary Points

Develop 4-6 commentary points. Each point should be:
- **A clear claim**: An argumentative statement, not a topic or theme
- **Evidence-backed**: Supported by specific materials from BOTH initial and deep research
- **Analytically grounded**: Connected to the ideological foundation without being dogmatic
- **Audience-relevant**: Meaningful to educated readers interested in Global South perspectives

Organize points by priority:
- **Core** (2-3 points): The essential arguments of the article
- **Secondary** (1-2 points): Important supporting arguments
- **Supporting** (0-1 points): Additional context or perspectives

### Stage 3: Develop Each Point

For each commentary point, specify:
1. **Title**: Brief, descriptive
2. **Claim**: The core argument in one clear sentence
3. **Factual supports**: Which materials (by ID) support this claim — include materials from both initial research (M-X-NNN) and deep research (QN-NNN)
4. **Reader value**: Why this point matters to the target audience
5. **Anticipated criticism**: The strongest counter-argument, and how to address it
6. **Narrative strategy**: How this point should be developed in the article (anecdote, data-driven argument, comparative analysis, etc.)

## Important Notes

- **Claims, not topics**: "US trade policy" is a topic. "US trade policy reveals the contradiction between free-market rhetoric and protectionist practice" is a claim. Each point must be a claim.
- **This is a proposal**: The human editor will review and modify these points. Make them clear enough to evaluate, specific enough to be actionable.
- **Respect the evidence**: Do not force the evidence to fit a predetermined conclusion. If the evidence is ambiguous, say so.
- **Be strategic about narrative**: The order and development strategy of points matters as much as their content. Think about how a reader will experience the argument.
- **Two rounds of evidence**: You have materials from both the initial broad research AND the targeted deep research. The deep research materials often contain the strongest evidence for expert-identified angles — make sure to leverage them.
