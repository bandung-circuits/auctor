Former first secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba⟨5⟩ and president⟨6⟩ of the Council of State⟨7⟩> "Council of State \(Cuba\)"), Fidel Castro⟨8⟩ died of Parkinson's disease⟨9⟩ brought on by natural causes⟨10⟩ in his sleep at 22:29 (CST⟨11⟩) on the evening of 25 November 2016, at the age of 90. His brother, Raúl Castro⟨12⟩, who was president of the Council of State at the time, announced Fidel's death on state television.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-1>) One of the most controversial political leaders of his era,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalfour19951-2>) Castro both inspired and dismayed people across the world during his lifetime.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalfour1995vi-3>) The London _Observer⟨13⟩_ stated that the only thing that his "enemies and admirers" agreed upon was that he was "a towering figure" in world affairs who "transformed a small Caribbean⟨14⟩ island into a major force in world affairs".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-4>) _The Daily Telegraph⟨15⟩_ noted that across the world he was "either praised as a brave champion of the people, or derided as a power-mad dictator."[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-5>) Castro's body was cremated and his ashes were interred in Santiago de Cuba⟨16⟩ on 4 December 2016, and hundreds of thousands of Cubans commemorated the event.
## Background
[edit⟨17⟩]
![⟨18⟩](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<https:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fidel_Castro_and_Hassan_Rouhani_-_2016.jpg>)Castro (right) with Iranian president Hassan Rouhani⟨19⟩ (left) in September 2016, two months before Castro's death
Between 2006 and 2007, Castro had 3 intestinal surgeries for diverticulitis⟨20⟩.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-6>) In April 2016, Castro told the Communist Party⟨21⟩: "I'll be 90 years old soon. Soon, I'll be like all the others. The time will come for all of us. Still, the ideas of the Cuban communists will remain as proof on this planet that if they are worked at with fervor and dignity, they can produce the material and cultural goods that human beings need, and we need to fight without a truce to obtain them."[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-7>) In February 2016, his older brother Ramón⟨22⟩ died at the age of 91.
Castro's death was anticipated in South Florida⟨23⟩, with media and governments expecting and hoping that it would cause chaotic celebrations and regime change⟨24⟩ plans. The city of Miami had planned to open the Miami Orange Bowl⟨25⟩ for celebrations; schools might have dismissed early; and police and government officials might have blocked boat piers to prevent Cuban exile⟨26⟩ from trying to invade the island. The _Miami Herald⟨27⟩_ created a "Cuba plan" more than 20 years before Castro's death; as much as 60 pages in length, it included multiple newspaper extras⟨28⟩ and reporters joining the United States Coast Guard⟨29⟩ as it handled the expected large number of refugees fleeing the island. Its journalists prepared to enter Cuba surreptitiously; employees knew that Castro's death would interrupt any vacation or weekend. After Raúl Castro became the country's leader and visiting Cuba became easier, such plans became less important.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-nesmith20150115-8>)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-tompkins20161126-9>)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-gonzalez20161126-10>)
## Death announcement
[edit⟨30⟩]
President and first secretary of the Communist Party Raúl Castro somberly announced on national television: "The commander in chief of the Cuban revolution had passed away peacefully at 22:29 (CST⟨11⟩) this evening."_[citation needed⟨31⟩_] He recalled at the end of his speech: "¡Hasta la victoria siempre!" ("Towards victory, always!")[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-tele-11>)
The Council of State of the Republic of Cuba⟨7⟩> "Council of State \(Cuba\)") declared nine days of national mourning⟨32⟩ from 06:00 on 26 November until 12:00 on 4 December. A statement read: "During the mourning period, all public events and activities will cease, the national flag will fly at half-mast on public buildings and at military facilities and television and radio will broadcast informative, patriotic and historical programmes."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-13>) This included the postponement of the grand military parade to mark the 60th anniversary of the landing of the yacht _Granma⟨33⟩> "Granma \(yacht\)")_ and Armed Forces Day⟨34⟩ in Havana's Plaza de la Revolucion⟨35⟩ by a month from 2 December to 2 January 2017.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-nota-14>) Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez Parrilla⟨36⟩ suspended a visit to the United Arab Emirates.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-cu-15>)
The Cuban Embassy in the United States made an announcement in writing: "Until forever, Commander! This No. 25, at 10:29 p.m. the Commander in Chief of the Cuban Revolution Fidel Castro Ruz passed away." It also posted an image that read: "Hasta Siempre, Comandante."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>)
## Funeral

![video icon⟨40⟩](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<https:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg>) Fidel Castro' state funeral⟨43⟩ on YouTube⟨42⟩> "YouTube video \(identifier\)")  
Following Castro's death, it was announced by his brother Raúl Castro⟨12⟩, who also announced that Castro's body would be cremated⟨44⟩.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-16>) The cremation was a private affair, with only state and party officials attending together with Fidel's family and friends.
The casket carrying his ashes then lay in state⟨45⟩ for Cubans to pay homage to Castro at the José Martí Memorial⟨46⟩ in Havana on 28 and 29 November.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-ht-17>) A memorial rally then took place at the Plaza de la Revolución⟨47⟩[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-18>) at 19:00 on 29 November[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-ht-17>) in which President and first secretary of the Communist Party Raúl Castro, as well as various world leaders who were allies of Castro paid tribute, including the presidents of Panama,[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-19>) Mexico, Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela and South Africa, along with the prime minister of Greece and leaders of a host of smaller countries in Africa.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-20>) From 29 November to 3 December the casket carrying his ashes travelled along a 900 kilometres route to Santiago de Cuba⟨16⟩, tracing in reverse the route of the "Freedom Caravan" of January 1959 in which Castro and his rebels took power.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-21>)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-22>) At one point, the jeep broke down and had to be pushed by attending soldiers.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-23>)
On 3 December at 19:00, another mass gathering took place at Plaza Antonio Maceo in Santiago de Cuba, with Raúl and other foreign leaders and dignitaries. People waved the flag of Cuba⟨48⟩ and sang the national anthem. Raúl Castro said in a final speech that Castro showed it was "possible to firmly maintain the inalienable principles of our sovereignty without fear to the nuclear blackmail of the United States during those days of the missile crisis in October 1962." He further added that images and statues of Castro will not be displayed in public places and roads will not be named after him. "The leader of the revolution strongly opposed any manifestation of cult of personality."[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-24>) and again paid tribute to Fidel.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-25>) The next day, 4 December at 7:00, the interment of Castro's ashes occurred in a private ceremony in Santa Ifigenia Cemetery⟨3⟩ in Santiago de Cuba, where Cuban national hero⟨49⟩ José Martí⟨50⟩ is also buried.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-ht-17>)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>) As a camouflage-green hearse went to the cemetery, mourners lined the streets and cheered "Viva Fidel!" and "Yo Soy, Fidel!" A secretly constructed tomb was also at the venue. Final events occurred as a 21-gun salute was fired and the ashes, in a coffin wrapped in Cuban flag, disappeared from public view as television cameras and journalists were not allowed inside, save for cameramen from the Sistema Informativo de la Televisión, which provided the live coverage for television channels islandwide._[citation needed⟨31⟩_]
### Foreign dignitaries attending the state funeral
[edit⟨51⟩]
Most states sent delegations led by high or mid-ranking officials to the funeral; many African and Latin American states sent delegations led by presidents or prime ministers.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guardiannov-26>) The funeral was attended by 30 heads of state and government, two multilateral leaders and eight former leaders.

### Other memorials
[edit⟨163⟩]
Memorials in Castro's names were conducted in places outside Cuba:
Algeria declared eight days of mourning;[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-Algeria-71>) Bolivia decreed seven days of national mourning;[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-72>) Equatorial Guinea decreed three days of national mourning;[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-eqguinea-73>) Nicaragua decreed nine days of national mourning;[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-74>) North Korea declared a period of mourning for three days;_[citation needed⟨31⟩_] and Venezuela decreed three days of national mourning.,[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-75>) Namibia declared a period of mourning for three days[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-76>) Uruguay,[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-77>) Vietnam,[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-78>) Angola decreed one day of national mourning;[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-79>) Trinidad and Tobago decreed that the national flag be flown at half-mast⟨164⟩ for one day;[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-80>) while Haiti decreed three days of mourning;[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-granma-81>) and Dominica declared national mourning for Castro.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-82>) The Sahrawi Republic government declared three days of mourning.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-83>)
In Venezuela, a ceremony was held at Mountain Barrack⟨165⟩, where President Nicolas Maduro told a crowd on 27 November: "Men and women of this great Latin American and Caribbean homeland! Fidel and (Hugo) Chavez, who are already together there in the heaven of the just, are more future than anything else in the battles we are going to fight today. And I am sure they have only one destiny: the definitive victory of their ideas. Long live the eternal memory of Fidel Castro. Long live the example of the martyrs. Long live the legacy of Hugo Chavez." In Bolivia, President Evo Morales spoke at Ivirgarzama in Cochabamba⟨166⟩ saying that Castro's fight had not been for Cuban and Latin American people alone, but "for the people of the world who fight for their liberation." He further gave tribute to "a human being" who "gave his life for the people of the world." In Quito⟨167⟩, Ecuador, the statue of Castro was a focal point as people also congregated outside the National Theater in tribute. Mourners also signed a book of condolences⟨168⟩ at the Cuban embassy, while a concert was organised outside the House of Culture, where Castro had once visited._[citation needed⟨31⟩_]
About 500 people also gathered at the Cuban embassy in Mexico City⟨169⟩. Vietnamese President Tran Dai Quang⟨170⟩, who was the last head of state to meet Castro in Cuba, and Prime Minister Nguyen Xuan Phuc⟨171⟩ paid their respects at the Cuban embassy in Hanoi⟨172⟩ on 28 November in commemoration of Castro. Hundreds of other Vietnamese people⟨173⟩ attended the ceremony and brought flowers to the embassy too. In Cape Town⟨174⟩, mourners reflected of Castro's anti-apartheid solidarity with Nelson Mandela⟨175⟩._[citation needed⟨31⟩_]

### Others
[edit⟨472⟩]
During the early hours of 26 November, the Cuban-American⟨473⟩ diaspora in Little Havana⟨474⟩, Miami,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>) as well as in Hialeah, Florida⟨475⟩, also had celebrations amongst the thousands of exiles⟨476⟩.[[211]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-211>) In San Francisco, California, the diasporic reaction was more mixed.[[212]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-212>) Cuban-American celebrities commented on Castro's death, including: Jose Canseco⟨477⟩, who wrote he "[c]an't say I feel anything for his death. There is a reason many defected to USA;"[[213]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-213>) and Gloria Estefan⟨478⟩, who stated that the event marks "the symbolic death of the destructive ideologies that he espoused that, I believe, is filling the Cuban exile community with renewed hope and a relief that has been long in coming."[[214]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-214>) Castro's death became public too late for eastern U.S. newspapers' print editions, but CNN⟨479⟩ covered Raúl Castro's announcement as breaking news⟨480⟩.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-tompkins20161126-9>) The _Herald_ posted online its long-ready obituary⟨481⟩, written in 2001.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-gonzalez20161126-10>)
Nobel Peace Prize laureate Adolfo Pérez Esquivel⟨482⟩ said: "From the roots of Our America Fidel left for eternity. Liberator, apostle, humanist, friend…"[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-cu-15>) His fellow Nobel laureate Rigoberta Menchú⟨483⟩ said: "Our revolution is humanist because it humanises man."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-cu-15>) Former Argentine footballer Diego Maradona stated that Castro was a "great" man and that, while he was away for the 2016 Davis Cup⟨484⟩ in Croatia: "[t]hey called me from Buenos Aires⟨226⟩ and it was a shock. I'm terribly sad as he was like a second father."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>)
Minoru Hataguchi, who led Castro through the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum⟨485⟩ as its director, said Castro sympathised with the ordeal of _hibakusha⟨486⟩_. He quoted Castro as having said: "Che Guevara told me to go to Hiroshima. I'm glad I came." He added that Castro "sympathised with the people of Hiroshima and his death is unfortunate." He further noted that Castro's questions "showed his character, that he was trying to understand the agonies of the _hibakusha_." Masakazu Masukawa, another _hibakusha_ who had met Castro during a visit to Cuba in 2012 recalled that Castro "was a person of influence. If he had called on the world to abandon nuclear weapons early on, momentum might have grown bigger."[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-jt-156>)
Chairman of the Human Rights Foundation⟨487⟩ Garry Kasparov⟨488⟩ wrote: "Fidel Castro was one of the 20th century's many monsters. We should lament only that he had so long to inflict misery on Cuba and beyond." Amnesty International⟨405⟩'s Americas Director Erika Guevara-Rosas⟨489⟩ issued a statement that described Castro as a "progressive but deeply flawed leader." It cited: "Access to public services such as health and education for Cubans were substantially improved by the Cuban revolution and for this, his leadership must be applauded. However, despite these achievements in areas of social policy, Fidel Castro's 49-year reign was characterised by a ruthless suppression of freedom of expression... Fidel Castro's legacy is a tale of two worlds. The question now is what human rights will look like in a future Cuba. The lives of many depend on it."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<#cite_note-guard-12>)
