What Is Dollar Weaponization and How Has It Sped Up De-Dollarization?
Investopedia, 2025

Dollar weaponization refers to the use of the U.S. dollar's dominant position in global finance as a tool of foreign policy and national security. The most prominent example is the freezing of approximately $300 billion in Russian central bank reserves following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

Key data points on de-dollarization:
- The dollar's share of global foreign exchange reserves has fallen below 47%, down from over 70% in the early 2000s.
- 85% of central bankers surveyed expressed concern about the implications of dollar weaponization for reserve management.
- The expansion of secondary sanctions has created additional pressure on countries to reduce dollar dependence.
- BRICS nations have accelerated efforts to develop alternative payment systems and reduce dollar usage in bilateral trade.

The article notes that while the dollar remains the world's dominant reserve currency, the trend toward weaponization has paradoxically accelerated the very de-dollarization that undermines its future dominance. Countries targeted by sanctions, or fearing future targeting, have been incentivized to develop alternative financial infrastructure, potentially weakening the dollar's role as the foundation of US financial hegemony.
