US to initiate Section 301 trade investigation into S. Korea, China, Japan, 13 other economies: USTR
By Yonhap
Published Mar 12, 2026

In the Federal Register notice, the USTR office said that evidence of structural excess capacity and production exists for South Korea through "large or persistent" trade surpluses while noting the Seoul government has acknowledged the need to cut capacity in the petrochemicals sector.

"Korea maintains a global goods trade surplus, led by exports in sectors such as electronic equipment, automobiles and auto parts, machinery, steel, and ships and marine vessels," the office said.

"Korea's trade surplus expanded considerably in 2024, reaching US$52 billion, up from a global goods trade deficit of $10 billion in 2023."

It also noted that Korea's bilateral goods and services trade surplus with the United States increased to $56 billion over the course of 2024 and remained around $49 billion over the four quarters through June 2025.

The USTR also said that the administration will initiate a second Section 301 investigation, likely no earlier than Thursday afternoon, to focus on bans on imports of goods made with forced labor and on whether other countries have implemented similar measures.
