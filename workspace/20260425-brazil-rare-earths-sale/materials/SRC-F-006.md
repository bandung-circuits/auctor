Brazil–U.S. Forum Discusses Cooperation and Investment in Critical Minerals
By U.S. Mission Brazil
18 March, 2026

São Paulo, March 18, 2026: Amcham Brasil, Citi, the U.S. Embassy and Consulates in Brazil, and the U.S. Chamber held the Brazil–U.S. Forum on Critical Minerals today in São Paulo. The Forum was the largest U.S.-sponsored event dedicated to the topic expected to be held in Latin America in 2026. It brought together more than 200 representatives from the public and private sectors to discuss business opportunities and potential cooperation between Brazil and the United States in the sector.

The Forum was supported by the Brazilian Mining Institute (IBRAM), and included the participation of representatives from the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation (DFC), the Export-Import Bank of the United States, Department of State, Department of Energy, U.S. Trade and Development Agency, and the White House.

The Forum identified opportunities to advance a win-win bilateral partnership that will support the development of the industrial and technological bases in both countries, including in areas such as geological mapping, mineral exploration, and industrial transformation. It reflected the interest of the United States in partnering with Brazil, the country with the second largest deposits of rare earth minerals in the world, to develop processing capacity.

Abrão Neto, President of Amcham Brazil:
"Critical minerals are at the center of the transformations shaping the future of the global economy. This is a decisive moment that Brazil and the United States cannot afford to miss to explore joint business opportunities and partnerships, strengthen more resilient critical mineral supply chains, and generate investment, innovation, value addition, jobs, and growth for both economies."

Gabriel Escobar, Chargé d'Affaires, U.S. Mission Brazil:
"Brazil has the opportunity to play a massive role in developing secure and resilient global critical minerals supply chains. The United States is already investing more than $600 million in critical minerals projects in Brazil, and we see the potential for billions of dollars of additional U.S. investment in this area."

Shawn Sullivan, Executive Director of Government Affairs for Latin America at Citi:
"Brazil has enormous potential in rare earths and critical minerals. Citi, a global bank with more than 110 years of presence in Brazil and a deep commitment to the country, aims to help attract investment, promote discussions and business, and support our Brazilian and global clients in identifying the opportunities that Brazil-U.S. cooperation on critical minerals can foster."

Pablo Cesário, Acting CEO of the Brazilian Mining Institute (IBRAM):
"Brazil must leverage its mineral resources as a catalyst for development, international integration, and leadership in the global energy transition. From the mining industry's perspective, the country should remain open to dialogue and cooperation with all nations, focusing on expanding investment, advancing technology and processing, securing market access, and strengthening the national production chain."
