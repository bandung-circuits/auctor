'China Shock'? No, China Opportunity! By Gong Rong (international affairs observer), February 10, 2026.

China's trade surplus reached US$1.08 trillion in the first 11 months of 2025. Yet some have seized the opportunity to hype up the "second wave of China shock."

From an economic perspective, the so-called "excess capacity" is a false proposition. Countries participate in international trade by virtue of their comparative advantages. China's competitiveness stems from economies of scale, a complete industrial chain and technological progress.

Chinese enterprises are promoting localisation of supply chains. Great Wall Motors and SAIC Motor set up factories in Thailand and Indonesia. BYD's Thailand plant has 54% local production ratio. Midea Group built Southeast Asia's first 5G fully connected air-conditioning factory in Thailand.

In the first 10 months of 2025, China-ASEAN agricultural trade reached US$51.3 billion (+8.9% YoY). China's fruit imports from ASEAN exceeded US$10 billion.

The Luban Workshop in Thailand adopts "academic education plus vocational training." All six majors have been incorporated into Thailand's national education system.

One-sided interpretation of trade surpluses, deliberate confusion between comparative advantage and unfair competition, amounts to a negation of the global market.
