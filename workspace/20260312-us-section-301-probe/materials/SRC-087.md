Hit By US Tariff Escalation, Indian Agriculture In Need Of Complete Overhaul
By Avanindra Nath Thakur and Amartya Jha, South Asia Monitor, December 28, 2025

India's agricultural sector contributes 16% of GDP while employing 46% of the population. Agricultural exports increased from approximately $48.8 billion in 2023-24 to $51.9 billion in 2024-25 (6.4% growth), with exports to the US growing 24% year-on-year between January and June 2025.

The US is one of the largest importers of Indian agricultural products in FY25 ($5.62 billion), accounting for 10.98% of total Indian exports. Key items include seafood (primarily frozen shrimp), spices, basmati rice, processed fruits and vegetables, and baked foods.

The US, Argentina, Canada, Ukraine and Australia have legally challenged India's market price support for rice and wheat at the WTO, stating it is around 87% of production value between 2021-23, well above the WTO's 10% limit. India invoked the WTO's peace clause for its public stockholding programmes.

From an Indian perspective, granting market access would have long-term negative consequences for employment, food security and price stability. The article calls for diversifying export destinations away from the US, shifting from raw goods to processed goods, and comprehensive support for small and marginal farmers.
