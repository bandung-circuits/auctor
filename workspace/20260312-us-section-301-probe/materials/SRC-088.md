Why Vietnam is in the middle of the U.S.-China trade war
By Lam Le, Rest of World, 18 April 2025, Hanoi, Vietnam

Vietnam's transformation into a global tech manufacturing hub started in 2008 when Samsung broke ground for its first smartphone factory. In 2018, when Trump launched a trade war against China, Vietnam emerged as a breakout star of the "China-plus-one" strategy. Today, most Samsung smartphones are made in Vietnam; Apple has moved production of iPads, MacBooks, AirPods, and Apple Watches there.

Trump slapped a 46% levy on imports from Vietnam when unveiling "reciprocal tariffs." This could prove disastrous for Vietnam, home to factories of Samsung, Intel, Foxconn, Nintendo, Lenovo, Luxshare, Goertek, and Pegatron.

The US announced a 90-day negotiation window, and Vietnamese authorities began negotiating a bilateral trade deal. Being a beneficiary of U.S.-China tensions comes with a price: "The reciprocal tariff on China still complicates the issue for Vietnam as China is Vietnam's leading trade partner and provides parts and components for Vietnam's manufacturing sector."

By mid-2023, a global demand slump hit Vietnam's assembly lines hard, with more than 45,000 workers losing jobs in the first five months of 2023, revealing how fragile hyperconnected tech supply chains are.
