Global Clothing Companies Need to Protect Workers as a Trade War Looms
NYU Stern Center for Business and Human Rights, April 25, 2025

Trump imposed severe tariffs on key trading partners, with top garment-producing nations hit hardest: China (34%), Vietnam (46%), Bangladesh (37%), Cambodia (49%). The US is one of the top markets for clothing and footwear, with 97% of clothing sold being made overseas.

The massive disruption poses a serious threat to millions of workers, mostly women, employed in garment factories across Asia. Tariff-driven factory closures are likely to trigger widespread layoffs, mass unemployment and economic turbulence.

Bringing manufacturing back to the US is largely unrealistic. US apparel manufacturing employment declined by 90% between 1990 (902,740 employees) and March 2025 (84,100 employees).

Early signs of reactive corporate response are emerging: Gap, Levi Strauss & Walmart froze Bangladesh shipments; Gap and Walmart asked suppliers to absorb tariff costs; Amazon cancelled multiple orders across China and Asian manufacturing hubs. This resembles COVID-era behavior when brands cancelled orders, and garment workers in seven major Asian hubs were collectively owed between $2.42 and $4.38 billion in unpaid wages in just the first three months of the pandemic.

The global garment workforce will pay the harshest price in this trade war. Companies must practice responsible disengagement as embedded in UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, OECD Guidelines, and the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive.
