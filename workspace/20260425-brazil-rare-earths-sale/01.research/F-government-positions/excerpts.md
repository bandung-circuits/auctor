## [M-F-004] Lula government formally rejects Terrabras at Palácio da Alvorada meeting
**Source**: SRC-F-001
**Relevance**: Documents the intra-PT divide and the formal decision point where the government chose private-sector over state-ownership model

> President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's government decided on Wednesday April 22, in a Palácio da Alvorada meeting, to formally reject the creation of Terrabras — the proposed state-owned company for critical-mineral exploration, refining, and processing backed by part of his own Workers' Party.
>
> The decision places the Lula government against the statist wing of PT and aligns with what the rapporteur of the pending National Critical and Strategic Minerals Policy legislation had already signalled on April 7.
>
> Terrabras was publicly promoted by the statist wing of PT, which argued that only a state-owned company could guarantee national control over strategic mineral resources during the current wave of geopolitical competition between the United States and China.
>
> The PT wing advocating for Terrabras drew an explicit comparison with Petrobras in the 1950s, when Getúlio Vargas established the state oil monopoly.

## [M-F-008] Brazil CADE opens antitrust investigation into Serra Verde deal
**Source**: SRC-A-006
**Relevance**: Regulatory agency's formal response to the acquisition — antitrust review under Brazilian competition law

> Brazil's antitrust watchdog has opened an investigation into USA Rare Earth's (Nasdaq: USAR) proposed acquisition of its only rare earth producer, posing a regulatory hurdle to America's critical minerals plans in Latin America's largest market.
>
> On Monday, the Administrative Council for Economic Defense said it has initiated proceedings to review the transaction, including the offtake agreement. The agency, also known as Cade, is responsible for evaluating business combinations and finding potential competition issues.
>
> However, the agency stressed that the proceeding "does not necessarily mean that the acts must be notified or that there are competition issues." In the end, the General Superintendence "may decide to close the case, to allow the transaction to be completed, or to open an administrative proceeding."

## [M-F-009] Rede Sustentabilidade party files Supreme Court petition on national security grounds
**Source**: SRC-F-007
**Relevance**: Political party within government coalition initiates judicial challenge to block the sale

> "For Rede, this type of arrangement can, in practice, shift the decision-making power over the exploitation of rare earths – a strategic resource of the Union – to a foreign group, without prior and in-depth analysis by the State of the impacts on the national interest. The party points out that Article 176 of the Constitution establishes that mineral resources belong to the Union and that exploitation must take place in the national interest, by means of authorization or concession, which would require more effective control by the State"
>
> "In the action, the party requests an injunction so that the Union and the National Mining Agency (ANM) present documents, opinions, and analyses on the questioned corporate operations. It also asks the regulatory body to detail the assessment of the national interest, technological impacts, and economic sovereignty, and that the related acts be suspended if the justification is insufficient."
>
> The Rede party was founded by Brazil's current minister of the environment, Marina Silva, and is part of the congressional coalition for the federal government.

## [M-F-019] Brazil's BNDES-Vale fund and R$5 billion transformation fund as alternative to Terrabras
**Source**: SRC-F-001
**Relevance**: Concrete government policy alternative to state ownership — fund-based investment model

> Rather than a Terrabras-style state vehicle, the Brazilian government is anchoring its critical-minerals strategy on two targeted investment funds. The first is a R$1 billion fund — split equally between BNDES and mining giant Vale with R$500 million each — focused on supporting smaller mineral-research companies across the country.
>
> The second is a R$5 billion fund dedicated to the transformation industry — that is, the refining and processing capacity that turns raw critical minerals into the specialised outputs used by electric-vehicle batteries, wind turbines, semiconductors, and aerospace applications.

## [M-F-024] Tricontinental Institute analysis: government slowness allowed market to decide
**Source**: SRC-F-003
**Relevance**: Critical Global South perspective on the Brazilian government's failure to act proactively

> Esse caso da empresa Serra Verde, com o DFC financiando e a USA Rare Earth comprando, revela exatamente o padrão que os americanos já usaram na África e na Ásia: entra como financiador, garante o fluxo da produção e compra quando o ativo está maduro. Enquanto o Brasil debate modelos e marcos regulatórios, o mercado já havia decidido.
>

>
> Antes de tratar com maior acuidade de aspectos legais referentes a essa transação corporativa, chama a atenção de qualquer leitor mais atento o teor da cláusula de proibição de venda da produção da Serra Verde para qualquer empresa do resto do mundo, inclusive para empresas brasileiras.
