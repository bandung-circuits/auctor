Beyond Rare Earths: China's Growing Threat to Gallium Supply Chains
CSIS Analysis by Gracelin Baskaran, July 17, 2025

As of May 2025, China had restricted export of at least 16 key minerals and alloys essential for semiconductors, defense systems, clean energy, and medical equipment. China controls approximately 98 percent of global gallium production capacity.

Gallium is critical for semiconductors (gallium arsenide and gallium nitride), 5G infrastructure, LED lighting, solar cells, and defense applications including radar and electronic warfare. Since July 2023 controls, Chinese gallium exports dropped by approximately 80 percent. The December 2024 ban targeted the U.S. defense sector specifically.

Unlike rare earths, where some alternative supply exists in Australia, there are virtually no near-term alternatives for gallium outside of China. Secondary production (recycling) accounts for less than 10 percent of global supply. New mining projects could take 5-7 years.

Gallium represents a more acute vulnerability than rare earths because: (1) China's market share is even higher, (2) there is no strategic stockpile equivalent, (3) substitution options are more limited, and (4) applications are more directly tied to military and semiconductor capabilities.
