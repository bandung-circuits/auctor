How the Netherlands compensated slave owners

Keti Koti or Broken Chains. 160 years ago slavery was abolished in Suriname. With freedom in sight, many were one last time ruthlessly reduced to mere property.

By Winny de Jong and Carola Houtekamer. 10 November 2023.

For 9 years, more than half of Jan Pietje's life, the 9-member Staatscommissie voor Slavenemancipatie, the Dutch State Commission for the Emancipation of Slaves, discussed abolition. And in 1862 there was finally a plan. First, slavery in the Netherlands Antilles and in Suriname would be abolished on 1 July of the following year. Two, slave owners would receive compensation for their loss of property. The amount was set for Suriname at "300 guilders per head". Slave owners who wished to claim that money had to submit two copies of the ownership declaration - or bordereau within thirty days of its publication to the government secretary in Paramaribo.

The Dutch state took its duty to the aggrieved very seriously. The government diligently set up a thorough and accessible process to claim compensation and committed itself to facile payouts. The Court of Audit checked if the money was lawfully allocated to the right people.

Verification commissions were put into place before the abolition to check that the names on the bordereaux corresponded exactly to the actual situation on the plantations. The Dutch state did not offer compensation for deceased property, property that had fled or property that was deemed to be economically worthless due to leprosy or elephantiasis.

Key examples of compensation recipients:
- Arnoldus and Amandus Bots from Helmond (also a member of the provincial parliament in North Brabant): received compensation for Esthers Rust plantation, 191 enslaved people at 300 guilders = approximately 55,800 guilders (651,000 euros today). Total from three plantations: almost 83,000 guilders (~1 million euros today).
- Hugh Wright (British plantation owner): Earned the most from abolition: more than 512,700 guilders from shares in three plantations (approximately 6 million euros today).
- Wilhelm Eduard Rühmann: received the fourth highest compensation, 307,260 guilders for 9 bordereaux (~3.6 million euros today).
- Jacobus Hermanus Insinger: received 339,682 guilders for three plantations (~4 million euros today). The payment was made through the Dutch National Bank. The director of the bank was Jacobus Hermanus Insinger himself.

The money did not always reach the beneficiaries. Many plantations were running at a loss and debts had to be cleared before the owner received compensation.

The Dutch state paid compensation for nearly 33,000 Surinamese enslaved people, almost 10 million guilders from the state treasury. In total, in the years following the abolition, the Dutch government paid approximately 12 million guilders to the plantation owners in Suriname and the Antilles. This is a substantial portion of the state budget — about ten percent of the total. But this could easily be supplemented with the profits from the colonies in the Dutch East Indies.

After emancipation, freed people were forced to work on plantations for a low salary for another 10 years under "state supervision," due to a rigged system set up to protect the plantation economy, which is why 1873 — and not 1863 — is considered by some to be the end of slavery.
