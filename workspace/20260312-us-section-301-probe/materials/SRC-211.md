India-U.S. interim trade deal triggers farmer concern

Published: February 9, 2026

On February 6, the U.S. and India announced that they have reached a framework for an interim reciprocal trade agreement. However, the announcement has raised concern among farmer groups and food security activists over potential increases in agricultural imports, including genetically modified (GM) grain.

India will eliminate or reduce tariffs on U.S. food and agricultural products, including dried distillers' grains (DDGs), red sorghum for animal feed, tree nuts, fresh and processed fruit, soybean oil, wine and spirits.

Raising concerns over the tariff concessions, farmer organisations have called for nationwide protests on February 12.

ASHA-Kisan Swaraj, a farmer network, said removal of tariffs on a wide range of agricultural imports could depress domestic prices, affect farmers and horticulturists, distort feed and livestock markets, and increase dependence on imports.

The group said imports of DDGs and red sorghum could affect farmers growing maize, jowar, and soybean used in fodder and animal feed. Soybean farmers are reporting distress in Maharashtra, Telangana, Madhya Pradesh, and Rajasthan.

The network also raised concerns about potential imports of GM food and feed products.

India intends to purchase $500 billion worth of U.S. energy products, aircraft and aircraft parts, precious metals, technology products, and coking coal over the next five years.
