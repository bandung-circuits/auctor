The PRC's Overcapacity Problem Depends on Who You Ask. By Ann Listerud, China Brief Vol. 24 Issue 21, November 1, 2024.

Senior Party and government officials have identified overcapacity as a major economic challenge, but state media have downplayed or denied its existence. At the Central Economic Work Conference (Dec 2023), overcapacity was singled out as a key obstacle. Premier Li Qiang referenced it twice in the 2024 Government Work Report.

In stark contrast, state media have asserted there is no overcapacity problem, in response to criticism from the US, Europe, and Global South countries (Chile, India, Indonesia, Thailand). People's Daily argued the US is "doing the utmost to exaggerate" China's overcapacity. Xinhua called it "a malicious means of using political tricks."

Chinese scholars' own definitions mirror Western ones: "too much product chasing too few buyers." The core cause is agreed upon: companies maintaining capacity "without worrying about making a profit, often due to a lack of economic pressure to operate efficiently."

Professor Zhu Ning of Shanghai Jiaotong University wrote that overcapacity arose when local governments all formed development plans centered around the same industries, spurring rapid investment. Government incentives create moral hazard.

With national-level directives pulling in opposite directions, regional policymakers navigate competing priorities. Straying too far to either side could jeopardize their careers.

The real impact is visible in construction materials, where the housing construction halt has led to sharp decline in secondary industries like sand and gravel mining.
