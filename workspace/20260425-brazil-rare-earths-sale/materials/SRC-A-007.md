Brazil rejects state minerals firm as US deal stalls and court challenge clouds Serra Verde sale
================================================================================================

Published on 04/28/2026 at 04:05 am EDT - Modified on 04/28/2026 at 04:00 pm EDT
Source: Acquiremedia / bne IntelliNews

Brazil's government ruled out creating a state-owned critical minerals company while a left-wing political party filed a Supreme Court challenge seeking to block the $2.8bn sale of rare earths producer Serra Verde Group to US-listed USA Rare Earth, Mining.com and Bloomberg reported.

The stance by the cabinet adds fresh uncertainty to an already fraught effort to forge a bilateral minerals agreement between Brasilia and Washington.

Industry Minister Marcio Elias Rosa told local broadcaster CanalGov there was 'no need whatsoever to create a state-owned company to carry out the exploration or processing of critical minerals,' pushing back on proposals for a state-backed entity that has become known informally as 'TerraBras.'

Finance Minister Dario Durigan echoed that position, arguing that strong global demand already makes Brazil attractive to investors without broad fiscal incentives, and that targeted tools such as the Eco Invest blended finance programme would suffice.

The government's stance comes as a proposed national regulatory framework for critical minerals remains stalled in Congress — with 13 related bills under review, according to the National Mining Agency — and as the cabinet presided by Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva missed its own deadline to deliver a comprehensive mining strategy.

Hours after the ministerial statements, the Rede Sustentabilidade party filed a petition with Brazil's Supreme Court asking it to suspend the Serra Verde transaction on national security grounds, arguing that Brazilian legislation offers insufficient constitutional safeguards over the exploitation of strategic mineral assets, Bloomberg reported.

Serra Verde's press office declined to comment, and USA Rare Earth did not respond to a request for comment.

The Serra Verde deal, which would create one of the few Western producers of heavy rare earths outside China, has become a flashpoint for wider tensions over Brazil's mineral sovereignty.

Serra Verde's Pela Ema mine in Goias currently produces around 100 tonnes of rare earth oxides annually, with output expected to reach 6,400 tonnes by end-2027 and includes a 15-year supply agreement with minimum prices for terbium and dysprosium directed exclusively at Western markets.

Analysts quoted by Mining.com warned that policy uncertainty, while not halting projects outright, was making capital allocation more selective and delaying higher-risk downstream investments.

'Without clear policies for adding value, building technology, and aligning mining with industry, Brazil may end up negotiating assets one by one instead of from a national strategy,' said Rafaela Guedes of the Brazilian Centre for International Relations.

Despite the federal impasse, the US is deepening engagement at state level, with Goias advancing a memorandum of understanding tied to the Serra Verde operation and other companies including Aclara Resources and Meteoric Resources securing US-backed financing for early-stage projects.
