How might tariffs affect the energy transition? July 22, 2025.

McKinsey considered three potential tariff scenarios and their likely impact across five clean-energy technologies -- solar, onshore wind, offshore wind, battery storage, and EVs -- projecting capacity outlook to 2035. Moderate tariffs alone would likely have limited effect on the adoption of clean technologies, especially in Europe. However, under sustained application of the highest-tariff scenario, by 2035, renewable-energy adoption could stall in the United States, and solar and wind adoption could soften in Europe.

Three scenarios: Productivity acceleration (status quo late 2024: 50% on solar, 25% batteries, 100% EVs from China); No real disruption (US 20% on China, 25% Mexico/Canada, 52% SE Asian solar); Global tensions escalate (60% on China, 20% others; EU 47.7% solar/batteries from China, 140% wind from China).

Key findings: Solar PV -- 60%+ cost reductions 2022-2023 mean solar capacity doubles by 2035 in any scenario. Global tensions escalate: 9% less US solar, 7% less EU solar by 2035. BESS -- increases ~70% US, ~6x EU by 2035. Global tensions: 4% less US, 10% less EU. EVs -- US at 14% penetration by 2030; EU 50% vs 41% in escalation scenario. US achieves 69% clean-energy mix by 2035 in productivity acceleration, 68% under tariff scenarios. But sustained tariffs stall expansion beyond 2035. Energy system costs: ~2% more US, ~3% more EU by 2050. Adoption of clean-energy technologies will likely take longer and cost more the longer tariffs last and the higher they are.
