American Hegemony at a Critical Juncture
Cadmus Journal, Volume 5, Issue 3

This academic article applies quantitative modeling to project hegemonic transition timelines, analyzing data from 29 historical great powers. The study identifies markers of relative decline across economic, military, and institutional dimensions.

Key findings include projections of US hegemonic turning points between 2032 and 2067 based on different models and metrics. The analysis draws on historical patterns of hegemonic rise and decline, including the Dutch, Spanish, Portuguese, and British empires.

The article examines indicators of structural decline including: falling share of global GDP (from 27% post-WWII to approximately 15%), declining manufacturing output relative to emerging economies, growing debt burden, infrastructure deterioration, and institutional decay. The study finds parallels between current US trajectories and historical patterns of hegemonic overextension identified by Paul Kennedy in "The Rise and Fall of the Great Powers."

The research builds on Giovanni Arrighi's systemic cycles of accumulation and Immanuel Wallerstein's world-systems analysis, while applying quantitative methods to test theoretical predictions about hegemonic transition periods.
