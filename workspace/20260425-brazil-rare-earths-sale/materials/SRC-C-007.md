# Mining Laws and Regulations Brazil 2026
ICLG - Mining Laws and Regulations - Brazil Chapter
Published: 16/09/2025

## 1. Relevant Authorities and Legislation

**1.1 What regulates mining law?**
Brazilian mining law is mainly regulated by Decree-Law No. 227 of 28 February 1967, which establishes the principles and guidelines for mining exploration, as well as the state regime and supervision ("Mining Code"), but mining activities must also comply with the Brazilian Federal Constitution, Decree No. 9,406 of 12 June 2018, which regulates the Mining Code, other laws and decrees, and all the ordinances and resolutions issued by the Ministry of Mines and Energy and the National Mining Agency ("ANM").

**1.2 Which Government body/ies administer the mining industry?**
The mining industry is administered by the Ministry of Mines and Energy and the ANM, through laws, decrees, ordinances and resolutions.

## 2. Recent Political Developments

**2.1 Are there any recent political developments affecting the mining industry?**
Among the main recent political developments affecting the mining sector in Brazil are the following:
1. approval by the Chamber of Deputies of Bill No. 2,159/2021 on 17 July 2025, which relaxes environmental licensing for medium-risk activities, including mining, potentially increasing legal uncertainty and socio-environmental conflicts;
2. the launch in April 2025 of the National Policy on Strategic Minerals, aimed at attracting investment and strengthening the supply chain of critical minerals for the energy transition, with fiscal incentives and streamlined permitting; and
3. proposed imposition of tariffs of up to 50% on Brazilian mineral exports by the United States, which may significantly impact projects reliant on U.S.-origin equipment and inputs.

## 4. Foreign Ownership and Indigenous Ownership Requirements and Restrictions

**4.1 What types of entity can own reconnaissance, exploration and mining rights?**
Any Brazilian individual or entrepreneurial company incorporated and based in Brazil can own a reconnaissance, exploration or mining permit.

**4.2 Can the entity owning the rights be a foreign entity or owned (directly or indirectly) by a foreign entity and are there special rules for foreign applicants?**
A foreign company cannot own a reconnaissance, exploration or mining permit in Brazil. However, a Brazilian company that holds such permit may be fully owned by a foreign company with the totality of its shares.

**4.5 Does the State have free carry rights or options to acquire shareholdings?**
The State does not have the right or option to acquire shareholdings. Article 176 of the Brazilian Federal Constitution and Article 1,230 of the Brazilian Civil Code set forth that mineral resources are property of the federal government. In addition, the Federal Constitution provides that reconnaissance, exploration or mining activities are of national interest and are considered activities of public utility. Therefore, the State already owns the mineral resources.

## 13. Constitutional Law

**13.1 Is there a constitution that has an impact upon rights to conduct reconnaissance, exploration and mining?**
The Brazilian Federal Constitution has an impact upon rights to conduct reconnaissance, exploration and mining. Mining legislation, including the Mining Code and general instructions, ordinances, regulations, and resolutions, must comply with the Federal Constitution. For instance, the Constitution sets forth that reconnaissance, exploration and mining activities are of national interest, and are considered activities of public utility.

**13.2 Are there any State investment treaties that are applicable?**
Brazil has signed several Bilateral Investment Treaties ("BITs") with countries such as Chile, China and Germany to promote foreign investment and protect investors' rights, including investments in the mining sector.

## 5. Processing, Refining, Beneficiation and Export

**5.2 Are there restrictions on the export of minerals and levies payable in respect thereof?**
There are only restrictions and special procedures for specific ores, such as those that are radioactive. In general, the exporting of minerals is supposed to be straightforward, and exporters also benefit from a tax exemption.
