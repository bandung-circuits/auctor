It's not us, it's you: China's surging overcapacities and distortive exports are pressuring many developing countries too. No. 3, November 2024. By Claus Soong and Jacob Gunter.

Yet middle income and developing countries are also dealing with China's surging exports. This edition tracks measures taken by some of the "G50" and Beijing's response, with case studies of Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Mexico, Turkey, Brazil, and South Africa.

Trade protection measures include: raising high barriers (Mexico); leveraging tariffs for greenfield investment (Turkey); mitigating lower-end production distortions (Indonesia); managing across a wider range of industries (Brazil); protecting from e-commerce giants (South Africa, Vietnam, Thailand, Brazil); doing nothing (Australia, Bangladesh, Tanzania).

Beijing responds far more mildly to developing countries. Beijing puts higher priority on geopolitics; values commodity exports from these countries; Xi's global south leadership positioning means softer responses; but defends interests where countries are "politically lost" (NATO members).

South Africa: China is largest trading partner. SA has trade defense measures in all four categories but Beijing has not responded publicly. China views the political relationship as paramount.

Brazil: Runs USD 65B surplus with China but growing dependency — China buys 36% of total exports (iron ore 40%, soybeans 35%). Beijing has not leveraged this dependency.

Mexico: Imposed tariffs on 544 product types, exempting trade agreement partners but not China. China's response has been extremely muted.

Turkey: 40% EV tariffs led to BYD's USD 1B investment. China took Turkey to WTO but without retaliation threats.

Southeast Asia: Vietnam has wide-ranging trade defense measures. Thailand crafted conditional deals — imported EVs must be matched by local production. Indonesia protects nascent industries.

Key finding: Beijing tolerates economic losses where it can make political advances, but swings hard against countries it has politically written off.
