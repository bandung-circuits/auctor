Tense Threads Under Trump: What the 2025 U.S. Tariffs Really Mean for Bangladesh's Garment Workers
By Farheen Jamil, Journal of International Business & Law, October 27, 2025

On April 2, 2025, the White House rolled out "reciprocal" tariffs, branded "Liberation Day." Bangladesh was assigned a 37% add-on to existing US apparel duties. After negotiation, the reciprocal layer was reduced to 20% as of August 1. Apparel already pays high US base MFN tariffs, so the effective duty now lands in the mid-30s.

Bangladesh's RMG sector employs about four million people and generates more than 80% of export earnings. The tariff climb-down came with conditions: Bangladesh linked trade asks to U.S.-friendly moves including a five-year commitment to import 700,000 tons of U.S. wheat annually, additional purchases of U.S. cotton and soybean oil, and a 25-jet Boeing order.

Most Bangladeshi garment workers are women. When orders pause, the economic hit shows up fast in overtime cuts, delayed wages, or temporary layoffs, and then at home, in food choices and school fees. Labor advocates cautioned that the original 37% shock was big enough to reverse social gains built over decades.

The article recommends: (1) publish a clear "content-for-relief" option, (2) tie tariff easing to specific labor-law milestones, and (3) move up the value curve toward synthetics and performance fabrics.
