## Principal Contradiction

The contradiction between Brazil's declared non-alignment on critical minerals and the contractual architecture of the Serra Verde transaction, which commits 100% of the country's only rare earth output exclusively to US-aligned channels for 15 years while prohibiting sale to Brazilian enterprises -- a contradiction enabled by 14 years of structural inaction and the absence of domestic processing capability that would give Brazil meaningful bargaining power regardless of which flag is on the shipping container.

## Commentary Points Covered

1. **The DFC Pattern -- From Financier to Owner** (CORE): Systematic global pattern of US state-backed resource capture across the Global South, from DFC portfolio map to Cato Institute's "state corporatism" critique
2. **The 15-Year Lock-In -- Non-Alignment Negated by Contractual Architecture** (CORE): Cascade of contradictions between non-alignment declarations and exclusive offtake provisions, culminating in deposit rarity reducing bargaining power
3. **The Sovereignty Paradox -- 14 Years of Inaction and Never-Brazilian Ownership** (CORE): Serra Verde constituted entirely with foreign capital, 14-year Chinese offtake without objection, BNDES financing foreign-controlled enterprise, PT protest only when output stopped going to China
4. **The Regulatory Vacuum -- Constitutional Principle, Legislative Absence** (SECONDARY): Article 176 unenforced, no CFIUS equivalent, new bill weakened by mining lobby, CELIS "CFIUS dilemma" for Global South
5. **Geology Is Not Destiny -- Deposit Rarity Does Not Confer Bargaining Power** (SECONDARY): Concentrate $5/kg vs oxide $50+/kg, MagBras pilot-scale only, CETEM synthetic solutions, 42 vs 55 MGOe gap, Indonesia counter-example
