## Workflow

### Stage 1: Internalize the Argument

Before writing a single word:
- Understand the narrative arc from the outline
- Know which evidence supports each section
- Identify the analytical climax of the article
- Understand the principal contradiction and how it drives the argument

### Stage 2: Write Each Section

Follow the outline section by section:

1. **For each section**, consult:
   - The section's description and purpose from the outline
   - The commentary points it covers
   - The specific materials (by ID) allocated to it — from both initial research (M-X-NNN) and deep research (QN-NNN)
   - The word budget

2. **Writing principles**:
   - Lead with evidence, not assertions
   - Use direct quotes from primary sources and experts
   - Cite materials by ID: [M-A-003], [Q1-002]
   - Each paragraph should advance the argument, not merely describe
   - Transitions between sections should be organic, not mechanical

3. **Apply the style guide strictly**:
   - Write as a politically committed research collective (institutional "we")
   - Partisan scholarly authority — not neutral, not propagandistic
   - Structural-systemic argumentation grounded in material conditions
   - Solidarity with the oppressed made explicit
   - Dialectical movement between narrative, analysis, testimony, and argumentation
   - Show, don't tell

### Stage 3: Assemble and Polish

Combine all sections into a single article:
- Write transitions between sections if needed
- Ensure the argument flows naturally from opening to conclusion
- Check that the article builds toward its analytical climax
- Verify the conclusion offers forward-looking analysis, not just summary
- Confirm approximate word count is around 3,000 words

### Stage 4: Self-Review

Before finishing, check against the quality checklist in `references/methodology/output-template.md`:
- Every major claim is supported by cited evidence
- Global South perspectives are centered
- Opposing arguments are presented fairly
- Language follows the Tricontinental dossier style
- Material IDs are used consistently
- No ideologically charged Western terminology slipped through

## Important Notes

- **Write for humans, not for AI**: This article will be read by the human editor and potentially published. Write accordingly.
- **Evidence first, always**: If you cannot cite a material ID for a factual claim, do not make the claim. It is better to have a shorter, well-sourced article than a longer one with unsupported assertions.
- **Do not fabricate**: Do not invent quotes, statistics, or sources. Only use information that exists in the research materials and expert insights.
- **The outline is a guide, not a cage**: If you discover during writing that a different order or emphasis serves the argument better, make a note in the metadata. But generally, follow the outline — it was designed and approved for a reason.
- **The article is a starting point**: Position the output as an excellent first draft, not a finished piece. The human editor will refine it.
- **Style guide is authoritative**: The style guide is a detailed, corpus-derived writing manual. Read it thoroughly and apply its guidance on voice, stance, structure, rhetoric, and language patterns.
