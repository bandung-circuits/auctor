The Perils of Not Negotiating as a Bloc: Global South Countries and Their Reactions to the Latest Trade Tariffs
By Liang Yan Fung, August 8, 2025

Unlike the EU or China, the Global South's lack of coordination in trade talks has led to high US tariffs. Indonesia received 36% before a cut to 19%. Thailand received 32%. Even Singapore received 10%. The Philippines received 20%. Malaysia received 25%.

The high rates show the inherent weakness of not negotiating as a bloc. The US has been able to negotiate with each country from a position of strength. In Southeast Asia, ASEAN did not negotiate as a bloc. In South Asia, hopes of a united front ended with the Pakistan-India clash. In South America, Mercosur could not present a united front. The BRICS summit in Rio de Janeiro also showed lack of unity.

South-South trade has doubled from US$2.3 trillion in 2007 to US$5.6 trillion in 2023. This trade can help replace lost US trade.

Global South states may utilise critical minerals and WTO frameworks as leverage. China used rare earths for its trade talks. Indonesia put rare earth access as a sweetener, getting tariffs cut from 32% to 19%. Other states pursued non-economic means — Mexico could increase drug enforcement cooperation, South American states could block undocumented migrants.

The Global South now has to act more like the EU. Individual middle powers can lean on their networks. The cocktail of resources, non-economic cooperation, and geopolitical support can bolster the Global South's hand.
