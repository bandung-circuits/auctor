China's Germanium and Gallium Export Restrictions: Consequences for the United States
Issue Brief, Stimson Center, By Sarah Godek, March 19, 2025

China's new ban on germanium and gallium exports to the United States builds on earlier restrictions in July 2023. Discrepancies between U.S. and Chinese trade data suggest materials are reaching the U.S. through third countries like Belgium.

Since 2023 controls, China's exports of both minerals declined significantly. Chinese data shows zero exports to the U.S. in 2024, yet U.S. data shows 26% of germanium imports (about 3,500kg) and 8% of gallium imports (900kg) still came from China. Belgium is the most likely source of reexported germanium — China's exports to Belgium rose 224% from 2022 to 2024.

Overall U.S. germanium imports decreased 68% from 2021 to 2024. Gallium imports fell 77%. Yet consumption has not declined, suggesting reexport, recycling, and stockpile usage.

The USGS estimated a total ban could cause 150% price increase for gallium, 26% for germanium, and a $3.4 billion U.S. GDP decrease. Should China crack down on third-country reexport, effects could be drastic. The lack of a gallium stockpile makes the U.S. particularly vulnerable. Full impact depends on China's enforcement of third-country controls and whether U.S. partners can add new production capacity in time.
